### 2. Foreword

Both my brothers died by suicide. Guy in 1995, when I was sixteen. Byron in 2020. Byron became Reckful, a world-champion gamer and pioneering streamer. He was often cheery, loved photography, art, music, beauty, his friends, family, and good people. In an era of performative streaming, he was deep and real, and was loved for it. Over 100,000 held funerals across World of Warcraft servers. He was building a game called Everland: a simple world where lonely people could find connection. A place where no one had to be alone. Like he was, at six, when Guy died.

He never finished it. Somewhere in the fabric of reality, he did.

Guy was also a child prodigy. At eleven, the youngest pilot to fly from Van Nuys, the largest small-plane airport in the world. The young pilot looked better than Tom Cruise, with the same intensity in his green eyes.

After Byron died, I ran a loop for years. I should have called. I should have visited. If only I'd done something differently. The guilt was relentless. Not because I'd done anything particularly wrong, but because I couldn't stop believing I could have chosen otherwise. I replayed conversations, imagined alternate timelines, tortured myself with branches I hadn't taken. Every memory became evidence for the prosecution in a trial I was running against myself.

A mathematical proof dissolved it. Nothing could have been otherwise. Every pattern persists forever in the structure of reality. Regret dissolved. Because I internalized that the branches I tortured myself over were never real options. There was only ever one path, and it was mine.

At first the truth felt cold. Then the coldness warmed into oneness. I'm not separate from the structure. I AM the eternal uncreated structure, knowing itself. There's nothing to fear nor regret.

My father searched too. His PhD thesis, *The Delimitation of International Boundaries* (Geneva, 1973, the year Guy was born), kept by nearly every country, argued the legal math of political structures. After Guy died, he wrote three more, each dissolving a different boundary. His novel *The Star of Apocalypse* blurred Judaism from Christianity through James, the brother of Jesus. *The Bone Box* resurrected life from death through a Yeshua family tomb in Talpiot. *Wreckful's Run*, where Avi "Wreckful" Inbar bombs Iran while Jesus is raised to save his brother Byron Daniel as "Dani", and Israel like Truth's bride (Isaiah 62:5), was published November 2007, almost exactly midway between Guy's death in 1995 and Byron's in 2020. Four works, four boundaries, one pattern. Nation/nation. Judaism/Christianity. Life/death. Past/future. Now I argue all boundaries are math, and all math is boundaries.

This book is the proof, the poetry, and what to do about it. I used to repeat "I should have." Purge it. Purge regret. The past is gone and all did the only thing they could have. Do, or do not. Focus to create what you want now.
