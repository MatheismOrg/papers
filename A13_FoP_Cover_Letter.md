Dear Editors,

I submit "The Arrow of Time Is Irreversible Computation" for consideration. The paper argues that the arrow of time is grounded in the logical structure of computation, not in thermodynamics or boundary conditions. Nearly all computations are many-to-one: 1+1=2 is deterministic forward, but 2=x+y has infinitely many solutions backward. The inverse function does not exist. Modeling, memory, and observation rely on many-to-one tasks. No system that models by compression can run backward through those tasks. The laws permit reversal; nothing in the reversed direction can observe it as reversal. The arrow is not a feature consciousness observes; it is what consciousness is.

The paper distinguishes substrate-level reversibility from task-level irreversibility, engages with constructor theory (Deutsch 2013), reversible computation (Bennett 1973), and the data-processing inequality (Cover and Thomas 2006). Approximately 2,500 words.
