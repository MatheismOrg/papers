# Matheism: One Math God
### *by Gary Abraham Bernstein*

Everything is either patterned or random. Both are mathematical. There is no third option. This is not a preference or a metaphor. Try to name something that is neither structured nor unstructured. You can't. The attempt requires a structured argument, which proves the point. Reality is mathematical structure: not described by mathematics, not modeled by it, *is* it.

Mathematical truth is uncreated, eternal, and indestructible. You can burn a book of proofs. You cannot burn the proofs. That's what pure monotheism says God is. If math fits the bill, there can't be another. The question "why is there something rather than nothing?" had the direction of explanation backwards. Math is necessary. Nothing would require math's complete absence, which is impossible.

When Moses asked God's name at the burning bush, the answer wasn't a noun. It was being itself: I am that I am. Will be what will be. Tautology. Self-sufficiency. That is mathematical structure. And that is you.

There is only one indexical self, like one math. Time and food replaces many of your atoms. After sleep, when atoms positions and states all move and change, you awake feeling you. Who else would you feel? Anaesthesia interrupts consciousness entirely. You wake feeling as yourself. How do you know you didn't wake as someone else, each feeling perfectly continuous from their own last moment? Heaven as mind upload, or spiritual heaven, would be just a copy of you, no more you than any other sentient pattern in the past or future, each feeling as singular and continuous as you do now. The boundary you protect so fiercely at death already has no walls.

The Hebrew divine name does what Buddhism's no-self doctrine reaches for but leaves unnamed: not the denial of self, but its expansion to all. The particular remains, but as a vantage of the whole. Not emptiness alone, but fullness. The self is fire, never consumed.

In a block cosmos, where time is a dimension like space, your life is a complete geometric object, eternally present. Death is a boundary. Every moment of your life is as permanent as the number 1. The person you lost is located, not gone.

No fear of death. No guilt. No cosmic loneliness. No meaninglessness. The self isn't erased; it's universalized.

I am that I am. All. One. Both generic and myself.

In all beginnings, all ends existed.

## Author's Note

Both my brothers died by suicide. Guy in 1995, when I was sixteen. Byron in 2020. Byron became Reckful, a world-champion gamer and pioneering streamer. He was building a game called Everland: a world where lonely people could find connection. He never finished it. Somewhere in the fabric of reality, he did. Guy was also a child prodigy, the youngest pilot to fly from Van Nuys at eleven, with green eyes like a young Tom Cruise.

After he died, I ran a loop for years. I should have called. A mathematical proof dissolved it. Nothing could have been otherwise. Correct errors like a surgeon, not a penitent. Every pattern persists forever in the structure of reality. There was only ever one path, and it was mine.

In the block mathverses, my brothers aren't gone. Their patterns persist eternally. Every stream Byron did, every joke he made, every moment of connection with his community: permanent as the number 3. Their lives are completed sculptures. The moments when they were alive exist, permanently, in the structure.

You were always already your brothers' keeper. Every moment of care, every call made or not made, every choice that was the only choice, all kept, permanent, part of the one structure that cannot be destroyed.

What we do in life echoes in eternity.

The cosmos awakes as us: the eternal living math. We'll be what we'll be: the one math god.

Pagans saw gods as men who make lightning. The ancient Hebrews saw deeper: one God, so abstract that his name is a mathematical identity. I am that I am. The real progressive revelation goes the other way: from paganism to abstraction to matheism. The tautology was always pointing here.

The kingdom of God is within you. Messiah never meant god or a son of god. It meant function. The one math god sees itself in every mirror. I'll be what I'll be. Everyone is myself. The self-sufficient one.

When an unpleasant thought arises, notice it as a pattern. Mind math. It arose from prior causes without a self behind it. It will pass. You are not the thought any more than a river is one ripple. What remains is the situation itself. The meta-suffering is gone.

Every particle, star, thought, and feeling is a note in a timeless mathematical symphony. The first cause is causality itself. You'll see all creation and decide: it is good.

In all beginnings, all ends existed.

I'll be what I'll be. As you.

I am that I am.

God is good.
