# Glossary of Terms

**A priori.** Known before experience, from reasoning alone. The PRD is a priori: you do not need to observe the universe to know that everything is patterned or random. Contrast with a posteriori, known from observation.

**Algorithmic incompressibility.** A sequence that cannot be described by anything shorter than itself. This is the formal definition of randomness: no pattern, no shortcut, no compression. A random string of 1,000 digits requires all 1,000 digits to specify. The digits of pi require only a short formula.

**Arrow of computation.** The structural fact that nearly all computations are many-to-one: 1+1=2, but 2=x+y has infinitely many solutions. The inverse does not exist. This is why time has a direction, why complexity grows, and why consciousness runs forward. The thermodynamic arrow (entropy increases) is a consequence. The computational arrow is the foundation.

**Block universe.** The view from general relativity that past, present, and future all exist equally, like locations on a map. Your fifth birthday is as real as this sentence. Time is a dimension, not a flow. Death is a boundary, not an ending.

**Boltzmann brain.** A hypothetical conscious brain that assembles by random chance in an empty universe, hallucinating an ordered world before dissolving. Whether Boltzmann brains dominate over structured observers is an open problem in cosmological measure theory. The generating theory is equally simple for both; algorithmic probability ranks theories, not configurations within them. Any cutoff measure imposed to suppress Boltzmann brain dominance is itself a specification with a K cost.

**Born rule.** The rule that quantum probabilities follow the square of the wave amplitude. If a quantum state has amplitude 1/√3 for an outcome, the probability is 1/3. Why squared? This remains an open question, though several derivations within Many-Worlds have been proposed.

**Chinese Room.** John Searle's thought experiment: a person in a room follows rules to manipulate Chinese symbols without understanding Chinese. Searle argued this shows computation alone cannot produce understanding. Under matheism, the room IS understanding at the system level, just as no single neuron understands what the brain knows.

**Collapse (quantum).** The idea that when you measure a quantum system, its wave of possibilities randomly selects one outcome and discards the rest. Physically undefined: no equation specifies when or how collapse occurs. Each measurement adds a postulate. Many-Worlds avoids this by keeping all outcomes.

**Compactification.** Curling extra dimensions up so small they are undetectable. String theory requires 6 or 7 extra dimensions beyond the 4 we observe, each compactified into a tiny geometric shape. The shape determines particle physics, but specifying it costs thousands of bits.

**Compatibilism.** The position that free will and determinism coexist. Your deliberation is part of the causal chain, determined by your nature, yet yours because your nature is doing the choosing. Distinct from libertarian free will, which requires uncaused causation.

**Conservation law.** A quantity that physics preserves over time. Energy, momentum, angular momentum, and electric charge are conserved. Emmy Noether proved in 1918 that every conservation law corresponds to a symmetry: energy conservation comes from time symmetry, momentum from spatial symmetry.

**Constructor theory.** David Deutsch and Chiara Marletto's framework that reformulates physics in terms of which transformations are possible and which are impossible, rather than what happens. A constructor is a system that performs a task and returns to its ready state. The reset is many-to-one, which contains the arrow of time.

**Dark energy.** The unknown cause of the accelerating expansion of the universe. Space itself is stretching, and the stretching is speeding up. Approximately 68% of the universe's total energy content is dark energy. Its nature remains one of the honest open questions. Simplicity dominance predicts the answer will be simple when found.

**Dark matter.** Matter that interacts gravitationally but not electromagnetically: invisible, but its effects on galaxy rotation and cosmic structure are measured. Approximately 27% of the universe's total energy content. Its identity remains unknown. Simplicity dominance predicts the answer will be simple when found.

**Decoherence.** The process by which a quantum system's possible outcomes become independent of each other through interaction with the environment. Trillions of environmental interactions drown out quantum interference, leaving each outcome isolated in its own branch. This is why we observe definite results rather than quantum superpositions.

**Description-space.** The space of all possible descriptions (specifications) of mathematical structures. A short description occupies a larger fraction of this space than a long one: each additional bit halves the fraction. This is why simpler structures have higher measure.

**Dichotomy.** A division into exactly two categories that exhaust all possibilities. The pattern-randomness dichotomy (PRD) divides everything into patterned or random. Both are mathematical. There is no third option.

**Dualism.** Any philosophy that posits two entirely different kinds of stuff: mind and matter, sacred and profane, natural and supernatural. Under matheism, all dualisms collapse. If two things interact, they must share structure. If they share structure, they are not two kinds of things.

**Emergence.** When a system displays properties that its individual parts do not have. No single water molecule is wet. Wetness emerges from billions interacting. Under matheism, emergence is coarse-graining: many micro-level details map to one macro-level description. The emergent property is real but not additional.

**Entanglement.** A quantum connection between two particles such that measuring one instantly determines the state of the other, regardless of distance. Einstein called it "spooky action at a distance." Under superdeterminism, nothing changes at a distance: the outcomes were determined from the start by shared initial conditions. Under Many-Worlds, both outcomes occur in every branch, and the branches correlate.

**Entropy.** The physicist's measure of disorder. A shuffled deck has high entropy; a sorted deck has low. Under matheism, low entropy is low descriptive complexity. The universe started orderly (low entropy) because low complexity is exponentially favored by algorithmic probability.

**Epistemic.** Relating to knowledge and what we can know, as opposed to ontic (relating to what exists). "We don't know which branch we're on" is epistemic. "All branches exist" is ontic. Under matheism, the ontic question is settled: all consistent structures exist. Most remaining questions are epistemic.

**Eschatological.** Relating to end-times prophecy: beliefs about apocalypse, final judgment, the end of the world. Relevant to game theory because an agent optimizing for apocalypse cannot be deterred by threats to its survival.

**Fine-tuning.** The observation that the physical constants of our universe appear calibrated for complex life. Change gravity slightly, stars cannot form. Adjust the strong force, atoms fall apart. Under matheism, fine-tuning dissolves: every set of constants exists as a mathematical structure. We observe the ones compatible with observers because we could not observe anything else.

**Gauge symmetry.** The principle that physics stays the same when you shift your measuring stick in certain ways. Electromagnetism is a gauge symmetry: shift the voltage everywhere by the same amount and nothing changes. The strong force is a color symmetry: relabel quark colors and nothing changes. Every force reduces to a symmetry.

**Gödel's incompleteness.** Kurt Gödel proved that any formal system powerful enough to express arithmetic contains true statements it cannot prove from within. Like a camera that photographs every corner of a room except the lens you are looking through. The lens is still in the room. Gödel found that mathematics always has more truth than any single system can capture.

**General relativity.** Einstein's theory that gravity is not a force but the curvature of spacetime. A massive object bends space the way a bowling ball bends a mattress. Confirmed to fourteen decimal places. Also shows that space and time are one fabric, and that past, present, and future coexist (the block universe).

**Generating rule.** The shortest description that produces a mathematical structure. The generating rule for the digits of pi is a short formula. The generating rule for a truly random string is the string itself. Under algorithmic probability, structures with shorter generating rules have exponentially higher measure. The generating rule is the structure's identity card.

**Geometric harmony.** The framework developed in A19 where the Standard Model's particle spectrum arises as geometric overtones above the metric bundle, the way musical harmonics arise above a fundamental frequency. String theory heard the right music and guessed the wrong instrument. The strings are geometric overtones: derived, not postulated.

**Heuristic.** A rule of thumb: useful and often right, but not proven. Before algorithmic probability, Occam's Razor was a heuristic. After algorithmic probability, it is a theorem.

**Holographic principle.** The discovery that a black hole's three-dimensional interior maps entirely onto its two-dimensional surface. More generally: any number of dimensions maps to one. Dimensions are how you look at the math, not what the math is.

**Hypergraph.** A generalization of a graph where edges can connect any number of points, not just two. Wolfram's Physics Project proposes that spacetime emerges from the repeated application of simple hypergraph rewriting rules. These rules have very low descriptive complexity, making them exponentially favored by algorithmic probability.

**Indexical.** Relating to a particular point of view: this I, this here, this now. There is only one indexical self because there is only one I-am-here-now at any moment. Under matheism, all conscious patterns share one generic indexical position.

**Instantiation.** The realization of an abstract pattern in a concrete structure. Under matheism, the distinction dissolves: abstract IS concrete. A mathematical structure does not need to be instantiated in some other medium. It exists as itself. The math is the territory.

**Kolmogorov complexity.** The length of the shortest description that generates something. The digits of pi have low Kolmogorov complexity despite being infinitely long, because a short formula generates all of them. A truly random string has high Kolmogorov complexity: no shortcut exists.

**Libertarian free will.** The claim that choices are both uncaused and willed. "Free" means uncaused (randomness, no will). "Will" means determined by a nature (determinism). The conjunction is a square circle.

**Many-to-one.** A function where multiple inputs produce the same output. 3+2=5 and 4+1=5: two inputs, one output. This is the structural root of the arrow of time, the Principle of Least Action, and coarse-graining. Irreversibility is not a physical accident. It is a mathematical fact about functions.

**Many-Worlds interpretation.** The interpretation of quantum mechanics where every possible outcome of every measurement actually occurs, and the universe branches. The wavefunction never collapses. All outcomes coexist. It adds nothing to the Schrödinger equation, making it the simplest interpretation under algorithmic probability.

**Matheism.** Mathematical monism or math pantheism: the position that all reality is identical to mathematical structure, derived from the pattern-randomness dichotomy. Not a hypothesis but a derivation. The math is the territory.

**Mathematical monism.** The position that all reality is one kind of thing: mathematical structure. Also called matheism. Derived from the pattern-randomness dichotomy: everything is patterned or random, both are mathematical, there is no third option.

**Measure.** The probability weight assigned to a mathematical structure in the space of all structures. Think of it as how much room a structure occupies in the space of all possible descriptions. A short description occupies a large fraction. A long one occupies an exponentially smaller fraction. Under algorithmic probability, simpler structures have exponentially higher measure.

**Measure-favored / measure zero.** A structure is measure-favored if it has high probability in description-space (short description, high measure). Measure zero means probability effectively zero: a structure so complex that its description occupies a vanishing fraction of description-space. String theory is effectively measure zero relative to simpler alternatives.

**Metric bundle.** The space of all possible metrics (ways to measure distance) on a 4D spacetime. A metric on 4D spacetime has 10 independent components. To specify which metric you have, you need 4 coordinates plus 10 components: 4+10=14 dimensions. This is the mathematical origin of Weinstein's 14D Geometric Unity. The metric bundle exists the moment spacetime has a way to measure distance. Zero additional specification.

**Microtubules.** Tiny structural tubes inside cells. Roger Penrose proposed that quantum coherence in microtubules gives rise to consciousness. Under matheism, this is one more mathematical explanation of consciousness: structure all the way down.

**Multiverse.** The set of all existing universes. Under matheism, all consistent mathematical structures exist. The multiverse is not a hypothesis but a logical consequence: any filter selecting some structures over others is itself a structure, so all filters exist, including the null filter.

**Noether's theorem.** Emmy Noether proved in 1918 that every continuous symmetry in physics corresponds to a conservation law. Time symmetry gives conservation of energy. Spatial symmetry gives conservation of momentum. Without symmetry, no conservation. Without conservation, no stable structures. Without stable structures, no observers.

**Observer-moment.** A single conscious experience at a specific time and place. Under algorithmic probability, each observer-moment is a prefix in description-space. Earlier observer-moments have shorter prefixes and higher individual measure, but later ones are more numerous. We find ourselves where these two pressures meet.

**Occam's Razor.** The principle that simpler explanations should be preferred. Traditionally a heuristic. Under algorithmic probability, it is a theorem: simpler structures have exponentially higher measure. Every unnecessary bit of specification halves the probability.

**Oikophobia.** Hatred of one's own home, culture, and civilization. The opposite of xenophobia: contempt directed inward rather than outward. Under the consistency test, oikophobia aligns with civilizations whose values contradict one's own stated principles while condemning the civilization that produced those principles.

**Panpsychism.** The view that all matter feels or has experience. Under matheism, the question is structural: does a system model its environment? Self-model? The threshold for feeling is open. The logical error of panpsychism is claiming consciousness precedes math rather than being structure.

**Parsimony.** Economy of explanation. Using fewer assumptions, fewer postulates, fewer bits of specification. Under algorithmic probability, parsimony is not a preference but a mathematical selection effect.

**Past Hypothesis.** The unsolved puzzle of why the universe started in a state of extremely low entropy (high order). Under matheism, it is a theorem, not a postulate: low entropy is low descriptive complexity, and low complexity is exponentially favored by algorithmic probability.

**Path integral.** Richard Feynman's formulation of quantum mechanics where a particle takes every possible path simultaneously. Paths near the classical trajectory reinforce each other; paths far from it cancel. What survives is the classical path. The Principle of Least Action is what remains when everything else cancels out.

**Pattern-randomness dichotomy (PRD).** The logical exhaustion at the foundation of matheism. Everything is either patterned or random. Both are mathematical. There is no third option. This is the proof that all reality can only be mathematical structure.

**Performative contradiction.** Contradicting yourself by the act of making the argument. Arguing that reality is not mathematical structure requires structured argument: concepts in logical relation, premises supporting conclusion. The critic deploys mathematical structure to deny mathematical structure.

**Philosophical zombie.** A hypothetical being physically identical to you but lacking conscious experience. Under matheism, zombies can be labeled but not constructed: if a structure were exactly your math, it would feel as you. The depth of the label is limited by contradiction.

**Planck scale.** The smallest meaningful length in physics, approximately 10⁻³⁵ meters. To inspect anything smaller, you would need to concentrate so much energy in so small a region that it would collapse into a black hole, destroying the target. The act of looking destroys what you are looking at. Below this scale, the concepts of space and distance may break down.

**Poststructuralism.** The philosophical school that showed all signs refer only to other signs in circles, with no ground outside language. It was right that signs are circular. It missed that the circular structure is the math of objective reality.

**PRD (pattern-randomness dichotomy).** The logical exhaustion: everything is either patterned or random. Both are mathematical. There is no third option. This is the proof that all reality can only be mathematical structure.

**Principle of sufficient reason.** The philosophical principle that everything must have a reason for being the way it is. Under matheism, it is satisfied without infinite regress: mathematical structure cannot fail to exist, and the measure over structures is determined by descriptive complexity. The buck stops at mathematical necessity.

**Qualia.** The felt qualities of conscious experience: what red looks like, what pain feels like. Under matheism, qualia are not non-structural extras. They are what certain mathematical structures feel like from inside.

**Quantum mechanics.** The physics of the very small. Particles are not little balls but probability waves, existing as clouds of possibility until measured. The Schrödinger equation describes how these possibilities evolve over time. It is the most precisely tested framework in science.

**Rawls / Veil of ignorance.** John Rawls asked what world you would build if you did not know who you would be in it. Under matheism, this is not a thought experiment but a discovery: you do not know. You are all of them.

**Schrödinger equation.** The single equation that describes how all quantum possibilities evolve over time. It is the core of quantum mechanics. Many-Worlds adds nothing to it. Collapse adds a postulate at every measurement.

**Algorithmic probability.** Simpler mathematical structures have exponentially higher measure: each additional bit of specification halves a structure's probability. Physics fits on one page because compact descriptions have exponentially higher measure. Occam's Razor as theorem.

**Self-modeling.** A system whose internal model includes a representation of itself. Under matheism, consciousness is the felt transitions of a self-modeling mind. A thermostat models temperature. A dog models its environment. A human models itself modeling its environment. Self-modeling is the threshold for consciousness, though where exactly that threshold falls remains open.

**algorithmic probability (Solomonoff, 1964; Schmidhuber, 2000).** The principle that simpler mathematical structures have exponentially higher measure. **Solomonoff induction.** Ray Solomonoff's 1964 framework for predicting the future by weighting every possible explanation by how short its description is. Shorter explanations get exponentially higher weight. It is mathematically proven to converge on truth faster than any other method. It is the foundation of algorithmic probability.

**Standard Model.** The theory describing every known particle and force except gravity. The entire recipe fits on a couple of pages: about 25 numbers. Change any slightly and atoms fall apart. It is the most compact successful theory in physics.

**Structure-space.** The space of all possible mathematical structures, each weighted by its algorithmic probability. Algorithmic probability operates over structure-space: it selects which universes have higher measure, not which laws emerge within a universe. **Superdeterminism.** The interpretation of quantum mechanics where everything, including measurement choices, was determined from the start by initial conditions. **Superposition.** A quantum system existing in multiple states at once until something forces a definite outcome. An electron can be spin-up AND spin-down simultaneously. **Supersessionism.** The theology that a new covenant replaces and supersedes an old one: Christianity replacing Judaism, Islam replacing both. Under the consistency test, supersessionism places group identity above structural truth.

**Thermodynamic.** Relating to heat, energy flow, and the statistical behavior of large numbers of particles. The thermodynamic arrow of time points from low entropy to high entropy. Under matheism, this is a consequence of computation being many-to-one.

**Transcendental constraint.** A condition that must hold for observation to be possible at all. Not a probabilistic filter but a logical one. Observers require pattern. ## Additional Technical Terms

**Action functional.** A single number that summarizes an entire path through space and time. For each possible path a particle could take, the action adds up the Lagrangian at every moment along the way. The Lagrangian is kinetic energy (energy of motion) minus potential energy (energy of position): the ingredient the action sums over. The Hamiltonian is the related function that gives total energy (kinetic plus potential) and drives moment-to-moment evolution. Nature picks the path where the action is stationary: small changes to the path don't change the total. This is the Principle of Least Action, and it reproduces all of classical physics from one rule.

**AdS/CFT correspondence.** A mathematical duality discovered by Juan Maldacena showing that a gravitational theory in a curved higher-dimensional space (Anti-de Sitter space, a universe shaped like the inside of a saddle) is completely equivalent to a quantum theory living on its flat boundary, like a hologram encoding a 3D scene on a 2D surface. Evidence that dimensions are representation, not reality.

**Algorithmic probability.** The probability assigned to a structure by the fraction of descriptions that generate it. A structure with Kolmogorov complexity K has probability proportional to 2⁻ᴷ. This is the formal basis of algorithmic probability.

**Anthropic principle / anthropic reasoning.** The observation that we can only find ourselves in a universe compatible with our existence. The weak version is a tautology. The strong version claims the universe must be designed for observers. Under matheism, both dissolve: all consistent structures exist, and observers exist only in observer-permitting ones. No design needed.

**Bayesian inference.** Updating beliefs in light of evidence using Bayes' theorem: posterior probability equals prior times likelihood divided by evidence. Solomonoff induction is the Bayesian framework with the algorithmic prior.

**Bell's theorem.** John Bell proved in 1964 that quantum mechanics cannot be explained by hidden variables (pre-existing values that particles carry with them, like a coin that is already heads or tails before you look). The intuition is natural: surely the particle "knows" its spin before we measure it, and we just reveal what was already there. Bell showed this intuition produces predictions that disagree with experiment. Specifically, when you measure two entangled particles at different angles, hidden variables predict correlations that stay below a certain limit. Quantum mechanics predicts stronger correlations. Experiments consistently confirm the stronger quantum correlations. Any theory reproducing them must be either nonlocal (Many-Worlds: all outcomes occur) or must deny that your choice of measurement angle is independent of the particle's state (superdeterminism).

**Bijection.** A perfect pairing between two sets: every element in the first set has exactly one partner in the second, and vice versa, with no one left out. Like a room where every person has exactly one chair and every chair has exactly one person. When two mathematical structures can be paired this way while preserving all their relationships, they are isomorphic.

**Bohmian mechanics / pilot wave.** An interpretation of quantum mechanics where particles have definite positions at all times, guided by a pilot wave. It reproduces quantum predictions but adds a hidden-variable layer (the particle positions) on top of the wavefunction. Under algorithmic probability, this additional specification makes it less parsimonious than Many-Worlds.

**Branchial space.** In Wolfram's hypergraph framework, the space of simultaneous branches produced when a rewriting rule can apply in multiple places at once. Branchial space maps structurally to quantum state space (Hilbert space). When branches reconverge, they interfere like quantum waves.

**Brane.** Short for membrane: a higher-dimensional object in string theory. Our universe might be a 3-brane (3 spatial dimensions) floating in a higher-dimensional bulk. Specifying brane configurations adds to the total descriptive complexity.

**Calabi-Yau manifold.** A six-dimensional geometric shape into which string theory's extra dimensions are compactified. The shape determines the particle physics we observe. There are estimated to be 10⁵⁰⁰ or more possible Calabi-Yau shapes, creating the string theory landscape problem. Each choice adds specification complexity, making string theory exponentially disfavored under algorithmic probability.

**Causal closure.** The principle that every physical effect has a physical cause within the same system. A causally closed universe cannot be affected from outside. Under matheism, all consistent structures are causally closed: no supernatural intervention is possible at any scale.

**Causal diamond.** The region of spacetime that a single observer could both influence and later observe, shaped like a diamond when drawn on a spacetime diagram. Some physicists use causal diamonds as a natural cutoff for counting universes in the multiverse, avoiding the infinities that plague other approaches to the measure problem.

**Causal invariance.** The property that the final result of applying rewriting rules does not depend on the order you apply them. In Wolfram's framework, causal invariance produces general covariance (physics works the same in every reference frame), which is the foundation of general relativity.

**Cellular automaton.** A grid of cells that update according to simple local rules. Conway's Game of Life is the most famous example. Wolfram showed that some cellular automata are computationally universal. Hypergraph rewriting rules generalize this.

**CHSH inequality.** A specific form of Bell's inequality that can be experimentally tested. Quantum mechanics violates it, confirming that nature cannot be explained by local hidden variables alone.

**Coarse-graining.** Averaging over fine details to produce a simpler description. Many microscopic states map to one macroscopic state: this is many-to-one. Coarse-graining is why thermodynamics works, why the arrow of time points one direction, and why simple laws emerge from complex substrates. Many-to-one is the structural root.

**Conformal symmetry.** Symmetry under transformations that stretch or shrink space but preserve all angles. A map of a city is conformally related to the city: the shapes are right even though the sizes are wrong. Important in quantum field theory because physics at very small and very large scales can share the same mathematical form. Structure is scale-independent: the math does not care how big you are.

**Copenhagen interpretation.** The traditional interpretation of quantum mechanics: the wavefunction describes probabilities, measurement collapses it to a definite outcome, and asking what happens between measurements is meaningless. Under algorithmic probability, Copenhagen is disfavored because collapse adds a postulate at every measurement.

**Cosmological constant.** The energy density of empty space, responsible for the accelerating expansion of the universe. Its observed value is roughly 10¹²⁰ times smaller than naive quantum field theory predicts, making it one of the deepest puzzles in physics. Algorithmic probability predicts the solution will have low descriptive complexity when found.

**Eigenvalue.** The specific value that a quantum measurement can yield: one of the allowed answers. When you measure energy, you don't get just any number. You get one of the system's energy eigenvalues, like a piano that can only play certain notes. Each eigenvalue corresponds to a definite state (eigenstate). Decoherence selects which set of eigenvalues becomes the classical reality we observe.

**Eliminativism.** The philosophical position that common-sense mental concepts (beliefs, desires, feelings) do not correspond to real entities and should be eliminated from scientific vocabulary. Under matheism, mental states are real as mathematical structures; what is eliminated is the non-mathematical interpretation of them.

**Epiphenomenal.** Having no causal power. An epiphenomenal property exists but does nothing. If a second ontological domain never interacts with the physical, it is epiphenomenal and unknowable. Under matheism, nothing is epiphenomenal because everything that exists is structure, and structure is all there is.

**Eternal inflation.** The scenario in which inflation, the rapid expansion of the early universe, never ends globally. Different regions stop inflating at different times, producing an infinite number of pocket universes with potentially different physical constants. Algorithmic probability resolves the resulting measure problem: weight each pocket by the complexity of its generating description.

**Flux.** In string theory, fields similar to electric and magnetic fields that thread through the curled-up extra dimensions, the way a magnetic field threads through a donut. Different flux configurations on the same geometric shape produce different particle physics. Each choice of flux adds bits of specification complexity.

**Functionalism.** The philosophical position that mental states are defined by their functional roles: what they do, not what they are made of. Under matheism, functionalism is correct but incomplete: the functional roles are mathematical structures, and certain structures feel like something from inside.

**General covariance.** The principle that the laws of physics take the same form regardless of the coordinate system. Einstein made this the foundation of general relativity. In Wolfram's framework, general covariance emerges from causal invariance of the rewriting rules.

**Haecceity.** Primitive "thisness": the supposed property that makes a thing this particular thing rather than an identical copy. Under mathematical monism, haecceities are substrate by another name. A property with no structural consequences is indistinguishable from nothing.

**Hamiltonian.** A system's total energy written as a function: kinetic plus potential. Where the Lagrangian asks "what path does nature take?", the Hamiltonian asks "how does the state evolve moment to moment?" In quantum mechanics, the Hamiltonian is the operator that drives time evolution through the Schrödinger equation: it determines what happens next.

**Hermitian operator.** A mathematical object with a special property: its outputs are always real numbers (the ordinary numbers on a number line), never imaginary ones (numbers involving the square root of negative one, which appear throughout quantum math but never in measurement results). In quantum mechanics, every measurable quantity (energy, position, momentum, spin) is represented by a Hermitian operator. This is why your thermometer always reads a real temperature. The possible measurement results are the operator's eigenvalues (the allowed answers).

**Integrated information (Phi).** Giulio Tononi's measure of consciousness: the amount of information a system generates above and beyond what its parts generate independently. High Phi means the system is more than the sum of its parts. Under matheism, Phi measures one dimension of structural integration but does not exhaust what consciousness is.

**Isomorphism.** A structure-preserving map between two mathematical objects that has an inverse. Two structures are isomorphic if there exists a bijection between them preserving all relations. Under mathematical monism, isomorphic structures are identical: the Identity of Indiscernibles. Two structures sharing all relational properties are one structure. Physical and mathematical structure are the same.

**Lagrangian.** A recipe for deriving all the equations of motion in a physical system from a single function. For a ball in the air, the Lagrangian is its kinetic energy (energy of motion) minus its potential energy (energy of height). Feed this into the Principle of Least Action and out come Newton's laws. The entire Standard Model, every known particle and force except gravity, can be written as a single Lagrangian that fits on a T-shirt.

**Landscape (string theory).** The enormous space of possible string theory solutions, estimated at 10⁵⁰⁰ or more. Each solution (vacuum) corresponds to a different set of physical laws. Under algorithmic probability, this vast specification space makes string theory exponentially disfavored.

**Measurement independence.** The assumption that an experimenter's choice of what to measure is independent of the system being measured. Superdeterminism denies this: measurement choices and system states share common causes in the initial conditions.

**Moduli stabilization.** The process of fixing the sizes and shapes of compactified extra dimensions in string theory. Without stabilization, extra dimensions would expand or contract. Each stabilization mechanism adds specification complexity.

**Multiway system.** A computational system where a rule can apply in multiple places simultaneously, creating branching. All branches coexist. This is the hypergraph analog of quantum superposition: every possible application happens, and branches that reconverge interfere.

**Newman objection.** M.H.A. Newman argued in 1928 that if structure is all there is, then any collection of the right number of objects trivially has the right structure, making the claim empty. The response: Newman's argument applies only to abstract labels you can reassign freely, not to the concrete relations of physics, where what interacts with what is fixed by the structure itself.

**Nomological.** Relating to laws of nature. Nomological necessity means something must be so given the laws. Under matheism, the laws themselves are mathematical structure, not imposed from outside.

**Observerse.** Eric Weinstein's term for the 14-dimensional space consisting of a 4D spacetime plus its 10-component metric bundle. The observerse contains both the stage (spacetime) and the scenery (all possible geometries on that stage). Under algorithmic probability, it adds near-zero specification beyond 4D.

**Ontic structural realism.** The philosophical position (Ladyman and Ross, 2007) that structure is all there is: objects are nothing more than places in structures. Matheism completes this program by explaining why structure must exist.

**Pocket universe.** A causally disconnected region produced during eternal inflation, potentially with different physical constants. Each pocket universe is one solution in the landscape.

**Prefix-free coding.** A coding scheme where no valid code is the beginning of another valid code. Like phone numbers: 555-1234 is not a prefix of any other number. In algorithmic probability, prefix-free codes ensure that the probabilities sum to at most 1, making the measure well-defined. Prefix-free coding is why algorithmic probability's probabilities sum correctly and why each additional bit of specification exactly halves the measure.

**Renormalization.** When you calculate quantum interactions at very small distances, the math produces infinities. Renormalization is the technique of absorbing those infinities into the measured values of mass and charge, like recalibrating a scale that reads wrong at the extremes. The fact that this trick works for some theories (the Standard Model) but not others (gravity) tells physicists which theories are complete at a given scale and which break down. Under algorithmic probability, renormalizable theories are favored because they work without specifying every microscopic detail.

**Statistical independence.** Two variables are statistically independent if knowing one tells you nothing about the other. Bell's theorem assumes statistical independence between measurement choices and hidden variables. Superdeterminism denies this assumption.

**Substrate independence.** The principle that consciousness depends on the pattern, not the material. A mind made of neurons, silicon, or light would feel the same if the mathematical structure is the same. Under matheism, this follows directly: the math is the territory.

**Swampland.** The set of effective field theories that appear consistent but cannot be completed into a full theory of quantum gravity. The swampland program attempts to shrink the string theory landscape by ruling out inconsistent solutions. Even if successful, the remaining solutions still require substantial specification.

**Tensor.** A grid of numbers that describes how something stretches, squeezes, or curves across multiple directions at once. A single number is a scalar: speed tells you how fast, nothing more. A list of numbers is a vector: velocity tells you how fast AND which direction, like 60 mph northeast. A table of numbers describing how a rubber sheet stretches differently in every direction is a tensor. Einstein's general relativity uses tensors to describe the curvature of spacetime at each point: how space bends depends on which direction you look.

**Unitary evolution.** Quantum evolution that preserves total probability: nothing is created or destroyed, only redistributed. The Schrödinger equation generates unitary evolution. Collapse violates unitarity by destroying information at every measurement. Many-Worlds preserves it: nothing is lost, all branches coexist. Algorithmic probability favors unitarity because adding collapse adds specification at every measurement.

**UV completion.** "UV" stands for ultraviolet, meaning very short distances and very high energies (by analogy with light: ultraviolet has shorter wavelength than visible). A UV-complete theory works at all scales, no matter how small. The Standard Model breaks down at the Planck scale. A true Theory of Everything must be UV-complete: it cannot produce nonsense at any energy.

## Supplemental Terms

**A posteriori.** Known from experience and observation, as opposed to a priori (known from reasoning alone). Most of physics is a posteriori: you measure constants, observe particles, test predictions. The PRD is a priori: it follows from logic alone.

**Category error.** Treating something as belonging to a type it does not belong to. Asking "what color is the number 7?" is a category error. The fine-tuning objection to superdeterminism is a category error: it confuses specificity (many digits) with complexity (long description). The digits of pi are infinitely specific but generated by a short formula.

**Conceivability.** The ability to imagine something without obvious contradiction. Philosophers use it as a test for possibility. Under matheism, conceivability is unreliable: you can conceive of philosophical zombies, but a structure identical to yours would feel as you. Conceivability can gloss over impossibilities that only become visible on closer inspection.

**Contingent.** Could have been otherwise. A contingent fact depends on how things happen to be. Under matheism, all consistent structures exist necessarily. What appears contingent (these particular laws, this particular universe) is an indexical accident: which structure you happen to be inside.

**Description length.** The number of bits needed to specify something. Synonymous with Kolmogorov complexity for the shortest description. Shorter descriptions mean higher measure under algorithmic probability. Every additional bit halves the probability.

**Effective field theory.** A theory that works at a particular scale but breaks down at higher energies. The Standard Model is an effective field theory: accurate at particle-collider energies but incomplete at the Planck scale. Under algorithmic probability, the Theory of Everything should be a short generating rule from which effective field theories emerge as coarse-grained descriptions.

**Everett (Hugh).** The physicist who proposed the Many-Worlds interpretation in 1957. Rather than adding collapse as an extra rule, Everett took the Schrödinger equation at face value: all outcomes occur, and the universe branches. His thesis was initially dismissed. Under algorithmic probability, his interpretation is exponentially favored as the simplest.

**Information theory.** The mathematical study of communication, compression, and noise, founded by Claude Shannon in 1948. Shannon showed that information can be measured in bits: the number of yes/no questions needed to specify a message. Kolmogorov complexity extends this: the information content of an object is the length of its shortest description.

**Knowledge argument (Mary's room).** Frank Jackson's thought experiment: Mary knows every physical fact about color but has only seen black and white. When she sees red for the first time, does she learn something new? Under matheism, she gains a new representational vehicle (a new mode of access to structure she already knew), not new information about reality.

**Modal realism.** David Lewis's position that all possible worlds are equally real. Under matheism, this is almost correct: all consistent mathematical structures exist. The difference is that matheism derives this from PRD rather than postulating it, and adds algorithmic probability to weight structures by complexity.

**Nash equilibrium.** A state in game theory where no player can improve their outcome by changing strategy alone. Cooperation, defection, and deterrence all have Nash equilibria. Under matheism, ethics is game theory: cooperative equilibria persist at lower complexity cost.

**Payoff matrix.** A table showing the outcomes for each combination of choices in a game. Deterrence works when both players prefer survival. Eschatological theology inverts the payoff matrix: an agent that values apocalypse cannot be deterred.

**Reduction.** Explaining a higher-level phenomenon in terms of lower-level components. Heat reduces to molecular motion. Under matheism, all reduction is structural: higher-level descriptions are coarse-grainings of lower-level mathematical structure. Nothing is lost in reduction except resolution.

**Shared fiction (Harari).** Yuval Noah Harari's concept that human cooperation scales through shared stories: money, nations, religions, corporations. Under matheism, these are objective mathematical patterns instantiated across multiple minds. The fiction is real as structure.

**Spinoza (Baruch).** The philosopher who argued circa 1670 that God and Nature are one substance: Deus sive Natura. Excommunicated for it. Matheism updates the move: not God-or-Nature but God-or-Math. Deus sive Mathematica. The substance is mathematical structure.

**World-model.** A system's internal representation of its environment. Sentience is felt transitions in a modeling mind. Consciousness is felt transitions in a self-modeling mind. The richness of the world-model determines the richness of experience.

**Analytic continuation.** Extending a function beyond its original domain using the fact that an analytic function is completely determined by its values in any small region. If you know all the derivatives at one point, you can reconstruct the function everywhere. This is the mathematical basis for the claim that every point contains every other.

**Cauchy surface.** A spatial slice through spacetime from which the entire past and future can be determined by the equations of motion. Initial data on a Cauchy surface fix the full 4D solution. In A20, the jet bundle at a point encodes the same information more compactly.

**Jet bundle.** The mathematical space of all derivatives of a function at a point. The first jet is the function value plus its first derivative. The infinite jet is all derivatives to all orders. In harmonic geometry, the infinite jet bundle above the spacetime metric forms the geometric tower whose modes are identified with superdeterministic hidden variables.

**Kardashev scale.** A classification of civilizations by energy consumption: Type I (planetary), Type II (stellar), Type III (galactic). Under harmonic geometry, reinterpreted as the highest derivative order a civilization can read. Energy consumption becomes resolution depth.

**No-signaling theorem.** The result that quantum entanglement cannot be used to transmit information faster than light under standard quantum mechanics. In the jet bundle framework, this holds at low derivative order. Whether it holds at high order is the open question.

**Novikov self-consistency principle.** The rule that in a block universe with closed timelike curves, only self-consistent solutions are physically realized. Applied in A20: a civilization reading its own future from the jet bundle cannot create paradoxes because its reading and any response are already part of the fixed solution.

**Tsirelson bound.** The maximum strength of quantum correlations allowed by quantum mechanics in Bell experiments. Stronger than classical bounds but weaker than the logical maximum. Whether simplicity-biased distributions over jet bundle modes reproduce this bound is an open problem.
