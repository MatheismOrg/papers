A19 protocol T31 (session SA73, overnight round 11, 7 November 2026, US Pacific). Hashed before any prediction.
Is T30's miss a selection effect? Same data and frozen formula as T30 (FROZEN_T26_candidate.json, eaecf32e...).

WHY: T30 found that the frozen formula's high-mass branch, the Moster relation inverted at its mean, overshoots the mean
lensing halo masses of SDSS red centrals selected by stellar mass (Mandelbaum et al. 2016) by 0.46, 0.74 and 0.97 dex at
10^11.29, 10^11.48 and 10^11.68 suns, while it fits samples selected as cluster centrals. For a galaxy chosen by its
stellar mass, the expected halo follows from the relation's scatter and the halo mass function, as in the paper's
sixth protocol: prior = Tinker et al. (2008) dn/dlnM (200 times critical, colossus), likelihood = Gaussian in log M*
about the Moster relation with sigma = sqrt(0.15^2 + 0.10^2) dex (Moster's 0.15 dex scatter plus 0.10 dex for
photometric stellar masses, the value the paper's sixth protocol uses for CLASH).

PREDICTION (diag_mandelbaum_scatter.py): for each red and blue bin (VAGC masses, effective redshifts), the posterior
mean of M200m (h^-1 suns, NFW with Dutton and Maccio concentration, converted with colossus as the paper does), and the
frozen formula with its Moster term replaced by that posterior mean (M200c), all else as T30. Mandelbaum's values are
lensing-weighted means, compared directly.

TEST
Selection reading supported if, for the three red bins above 10^11.2 suns, the scatter-aware formula is within 0.2 dex
or twice the error of every measured value, where the mean inversion (T30) was not. Reported for all 14 bins. This
changes no score of the benchmark; the paper's sixth protocol already showed the scatter-aware inversion worsens the
cluster-selected samples (0.29 against 0.19 dex over the 70 MaNGA and CLASH systems).
