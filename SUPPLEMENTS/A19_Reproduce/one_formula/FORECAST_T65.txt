hand. What the runs gave: among disks the amount of gas does not matter (the gate "any cold gas" does as well as the gas
fraction: held-out all points 0.189 against 0.193 dex, range -0.009 to +0.001); for the eight dwarf spheroidals with a
dark-matter density at 150 pc from Read, Walker and Steger (2019), the formula's cusps give 0.21 dex, the published

times, history and cusps are level and self-interaction is 25 percent worse. Verdict under the protocol: not decided.

This is the forecast the formula is most likely to fail. The final formula gives a gas-free dwarf a cusp whatever its
history: every coefficient frozen (A, B, C = 11.7481, 0.8178, -0.7119; log M_t = 11.339; the core zero without gas), its
dark-matter density at radius r is the NFW density of its halo (t65/lib65.py, reading G). On gas-free dwarf galaxies that
are not among Read et al.'s eight, whose stars formed over at least 9 Gyr (star formation ended less than 4.8 Gyr ago),
each with a stellar mass, a half-light radius and a dark-matter density measured inside 0.3 half-light radii: the mean of
log10(measured / formula) over at least four such galaxies lies above -0.20.
If it lies at or below -0.20, as Fornax does (-0.51), the core follows the history of star formation: the gate must be
rebuilt on history, and the published recipe (reading H1, n = tanh(0.04 t_SF / t_dyn), r_c = 1.75 R_1/2) is the first
candidate. If it lies above -0.20 while the same dwarfs' densities lie above those of self-interaction at 1 cm^2/g,
gas-free dwarfs keep their cusps and the gas gate stands.
