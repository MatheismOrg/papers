A19 HALO RULES AGAINST GALAXY-GALAXY LENSING OF SDSS CENTRAL GALAXIES
PROTOCOL VERSION 8
Written 31 October 2026, 08:51 US Pacific, by Claude (Opus 5.5), session SA73, turn 7, for Gary Abraham Bernstein.

PURPOSE
The stellar rule was calibrated on rotation curves, which reach a few tens of kpc; galaxy-galaxy lensing weighs
the whole halo. Mandelbaum et al. (2016) measured the mean halo masses of red and blue central galaxies in bins of
stellar mass. This protocol (1) scores the stellar rule, frozen on the disks, against the blue (star-forming)
centrals, the kind of galaxy it was calibrated on; (2) tests the prediction recorded in turn 6 for the exploratory
rule that applies Moster's bend to early-type centrals, namely that an early type sits in a halo b(M*) times
heavier than a disk of the same stellar mass and size, with b about 6 to 16 near 10^11 suns; (3) reports the
stellar rule and the Moster relation against the red centrals as diagnostics.

WHAT WAS ALREADY SEEN
The lensing table (data/Lensing/mandelbaum2016_central_halos.csv, transcribed today). Before writing this
protocol a rough hand estimate suggested that the stellar rule lies within about 0.2 dex of the blue masses near
10^10.85 and 10^11.29 suns and below the red masses above 10^11.2, and that b exceeds the measured red-to-blue
ratios above 10^10.8. The turn 6 prediction for b was stated in that turn's reply and handoff before the lensing
numbers were read. No lensing value entered any rule.

DATA
data/Lensing/mandelbaum2016_central_halos.csv; the 149 SPARC disks (data/SPARC) for the size and HI scalings;
blind_test/frozen_disk_rules.json for A, B and C.

METHOD
(1) A typical disk at a given stellar mass: least-squares fits over the 149 disks, each weighted equally, of
    log R_e = p0 + p1 log M* and log M_HI = q0 + q1 log M*, with M* = 0.5 L_3.6 (the paper's convention).
(2) The stellar rule for blue centrals at each bin's effective VAGC stellar mass: M_ref = M_p/f_b +
    10^A (M*/10^10)^B (R_e/3 kpc)^C, with R_e and M_HI from (1) and M_p = M* + 1.33 M_HI. M_ref is M_200c in
    suns for h = 0.671 with the rule's concentration c = 10^0.905 (M_ref h / 10^12)^-0.101; it is converted to
    M_200m at the bin's effective redshift for an NFW halo of that concentration (colossus, cosmology H0 = 67.1,
    Omega_m = 0.3175), and to h^-1 suns with h = 0.673, the lensing paper's units.
(3) The stellar rule for red centrals, as a diagnostic: no gas (M_p = M*) and two sizes that bracket early types,
    the disk size of (1) and half of it.
(4) The Moster relation (with its redshift terms, as in protocol 5: M_200c in suns, Dutton and Maccio
    concentration at the bin's effective redshift), converted the same way, for both colors.
(5) The turn 6 prediction: log b(M*, z) at the red bin's effective stellar mass and redshift, where b is the
    Moster relation divided by its own low-mass power law, against the measured log ratio of the red to the blue
    mean halo mass in the same bin, with error sigma = (sigma_red^2 + sigma_blue^2)^(1/2), each sigma the mean of
    its upper and lower errors.
Sensitivity: the MPA/JHU stellar masses in place of the VAGC ones; no gas term for blue centrals; disk sizes
0.15 dex larger and smaller.
The lensing masses are means over each bin; the rule predicts a typical galaxy. Scatter at fixed stellar mass
raises a mean above the typical value by roughly 0.05 to 0.1 dex. This is reported and not corrected.

OUTCOMES
(A) The stellar rule is called consistent with the lensing masses of star-forming centrals if, in each of the
    five blue bins with effective stellar mass below 10^11.4 suns, its prediction lies within 0.2 dex of the
    measured mean, or within twice the measured error where that is larger.
(B) The turn 6 type-bend rule is called refuted at galaxy scale if log b exceeds the measured log ratio by more
    than three sigma in either bin with effective stellar mass between 10^10.7 and 10^11.2 suns.
(C) and (D), the red-central diagnostics and the Moster relation, are reported without a criterion.
