Choices and limitations

The main quantum closure and five lambda choices were written down in
PROTOCOL.txt before the numerical scan. The same-dust comparison,
finite-difference Ward check, covariant-tensor check and Casimir check
were added after the initial run, as explicit verification and scope
checks. They are not represented as blinded empirical evidence.

The zero total Box R coefficient was chosen to make a minimal closed
background evolution system. It is a physical parameter choice once
gravity is dynamical. Its stability under restoring a nonzero R^2
coupling has NOT been tested. The conformal-vacuum state is fixed in
that same renormalization prescription; positive radiation integration
constants are reported separately.

The free SM-count calculation is a benchmark, not an interacting SM
implementation. It assumes conformal scalar curvature coupling and
neglects masses, beta-function terms, scattering and symmetry breaking.
The area parameter alpha is not derived. Near-Planck-curvature examples
are formal semiclassical solutions, not evidence of approximation
control over quantum-gravity corrections.

Implementation correction: the first bracketing call used the exact
classical turning-threshold root as an endpoint; floating-point rounding
made its sign unreliable at lambda=0. Moving the bracket to y=0.5
fixed the endpoint issue. No physical equation or test parameter was
changed. Final run logs contain all successful results and the retained
physical failure control.

The free conformal source has vacuum polarization/anomaly stress. It
does not create conformal particles in the conformal vacuum. No claim
of particle-production dynamics should be inferred from the bounce.

The time integrator is standard implicit midpoint. Its convergence and
small constraint drift characterize the implementation; they are not
ToE evidence or a derivation of fundamental discrete space or time.

The response bound is an exact homogeneous algebraic condition, not a
full PDE hyperbolicity theorem. The scalar curvature combination Phi
being bounded alone does not bound all curvature: the D-dependent
curvature and the excessive-coupling failure are explicitly checked.

Main improvement worth retaining: a conserved four-dimensional quantum
source and a self-consistent nonsingular cosmological sector with a
conditional analytic curvature bound. Main unresolved issue: robustness
to the independent curvature coupling and to spatial inhomogeneity.
