MASS COIN AND GEODESIC CONTROL: INITIAL AUDIT

Leading massive continuum limit
-------------------------------
The exterior half-coin placement is correct. Let Ukin(a) be one complete
calibrated kinetic palindrome and dt=nu*a. In the smooth cell description,
Ukin=I-i dt Hkin+O(a^2). With Mhalf=exp[-i dt m N sigma_x/2],
    Mhalf Ukin Mhalf = I-i dt(Hkin+mN sigma_x)+O(a^2).
There is no leading mass conjugation by a finite substep rotation because the
complete zero-cell-momentum kinetic block is the identity. Placing coins inside
its individual even/odd substeps would require a different derivation.

In a two-site cell with two internal spin components,
    alpha=sigma_x(sublattice) tensor sigma_z(spin),
    beta=I(sublattice) tensor sigma_x(spin).
These matrices anticommute. The smooth positive-sublattice branch gives the
intended two-spinor Dirac Hamiltonian. The other parity branch remains in the
regulator spectrum. Exterior coins do not solve species doubling.

At constant lapse and exactly zero momentum, the entire kinetic block cancels
and the step is exactly exp(-i mN dt sigma_x). Thus its rest-mass coupling has
no sine factor analogous to the uncalibrated velocity defect. At finite
momentum, finite dt and finite m, regulator errors remain; the assertion is
about the controlled continuum limit, not an exact all-momentum Dirac law.

For varying N, neighboring site mass coefficients differ by O(a), and each mass
coin differs from the identity by O(dt). Their cell-position correction is
therefore O(a^2) per step. It does not change the leading lapse-scaled mass,
although convergence order must still be measured for the full massive rule.

Classical geodesic reference
---------------------------
The positive principal-symbol energy is H_ray=N(x)*sqrt(p^2+m^2). Its Hamilton
equations in coordinate time are
    xdot=N p/sqrt(p^2+m^2),
    pdot=-N' sqrt(p^2+m^2).
The Killing energy N sqrt(p^2+m^2) is conserved. Define eta=asinh(p/m). Then
    xdot=N tanh(eta),
    etadot=-N',
    E/m=N cosh(eta)=constant.
This representation makes mass independence explicit for identical initial
position and local velocity tanh(eta). Equivalently,
    xddot=2(N'/N) xdot^2-N N'.
At rest, xddot=-N N'. This is the timelike geodesic equation for the supplied
static metric in coordinate time, not a separate Newtonian assumption.

Recommended genuine packet-versus-ray test
-----------------------------------------
Use an evolved positive-energy massive packet localized well inside a periodic
coordinate chart, and compare its density centroid with an independently
integrated ray. Prepare different masses with the same initial rapidity,
p0=m*sinh(eta0), rather than the same momentum. Keep the packet narrower than
the lapse variation scale and broader than its Compton scale, m*sigma_x>>1.

A Fourier envelope times the positive-energy spinor of p*sigma_z+m*sigma_x
suppresses unwanted flat-background negative-band contamination. In varying N
it remains an approximate band preparation; its status should be declared.
Using only the central-momentum spinor is also controlled when delta_p/m is
small, but has larger negative-band contamination and possible zitterbewegung.

Compare position density/centroid and width at several times. Avoid interpreting
the lattice-generator current as the exact split-circuit integrated flux. On a
ring, use an unwrapped local chart or circular position statistics so a packet
crossing the coordinate cut does not acquire an artificial centroid jump.

Identical finite-width quantum packets at different masses need not have
identical centroids or widths: diffraction, momentum uncertainty, negative-band
contamination and averaging of a spatially varying force are mass-dependent.
Agreement with one point geodesic should be claimed only in a controlled WKB
regime. At fixed width, increasing m alone can leave a finite-width averaging
floor. A joint narrow-packet/classical limit, for example sigma_x proportional
to m^(-1/4), makes sigma_x->0 while m*sigma_x^2 grows, reducing both spatial
averaging and dispersion. Grid/time refinement must independently control the
rapid carrier and mass phases.

An alternative rigorous comparison is a classical phase-space ensemble matched
to the packet, alongside the central ray. This separates ordinary finite-width
averaging from numerical error or a failure of the candidate metric coupling.
