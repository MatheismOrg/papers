# Extending the acceleration search: constraints and independent inputs

Research assessment, 27 October 2026. These are constraints and candidate tests, not additional completed data fits. The Solar-System calculation supplied separately is an asymptotic calculation only.

## What must be predicted at each scale

| Domain | Measured quantity and relevant scale | Primary source / empirical finding | What a candidate must supply; why a scalar snapshot fit is insufficient |
|---|---|---|---|
| Planetary Solar System | Spacecraft range and Doppler, primarily Saturn's orbit over nine Cassini years; an anomalous Solar-System tidal quadrupole | Hees et al. (2014), PRD 89, 102002, measured Q2=(3 +/- 3) x 10^-27 s^-2. Desmond, Hees & Famaey (2024) jointly studied Cassini, SPARC and wide binaries within AQUAL/QUMOND and found model-dependent tension. | Specify the vector field, Galactic external-field boundary conditions and planetary dynamics; refit ephemerides/nuisance parameters or use an appropriate published likelihood. A high-internal-acceleration Newtonian limit alone does not determine the external-field quadrupole. The reported tension depends on interpolation family, geometry and mass-to-light assumptions; it is not a rejection of every conceivable modified gravity. |
| Outer Solar System / wide binaries | Relative velocities versus projected separations, e.g. 1-30 kAU; internal accelerations around and below a0, in the Milky Way external field | Cookson et al. (2026), arXiv:2602.24035 / MNRAS stag342, find no MOND-like enhancement in a quality-controlled Gaia sample. Chae et al. (2026), arXiv:2601.21728v3, report an anomaly in 36 binaries with accurate 3D velocities, dominated by two systems. | Model eccentricities, orbital phases, projection, masses, selection, close companions and chance associations, with the theory's Galactic external-field response. The current literature is disputed; do not turn either group's headline into a settled acceleration data point. |
| Globular clusters | Resolved stellar line-of-sight velocities and surface-brightness profiles in a stellar system | Ibata et al. (2011), arXiv:1106.4909, show that NGC 2419 strongly distinguishes assumed dynamical models; even isotropic Newtonian models fail while anisotropic models can fit. Sanders (2011), arXiv:1107.4953, disputes the MOND interpretation using polytropes; Ibata et al. (2011), arXiv:1110.3892, answer with additional fits. | Predict projected velocity distributions using a distribution function or Jeans analysis with orbital anisotropy, M/L and equilibrium assumptions. There is no direct replacement of sigma_los by circular speed. Useful as a controlled stellar-dynamics target, not a clean one-number universal constraint. |
| Early universe / CMB | Temperature, polarization and lensing angular power spectra, including acoustic-peak pattern and integrated effects | Planck Collaboration (2020), Planck 2018 VI, arXiv:1807.06209, obtains Omega_c h^2=0.120 +/- 0.001 in base LambdaCDM, with temperature, polarization and lensing consistency. | Predict expansion history, photon-baryon dynamics, perturbation evolution, recombination and lensing transfer functions. The quoted density is model-inferred, not an independent gbar-gobs point. A static acceleration rule does not define those ingredients. |
| BAO cosmological distances | Redshift-binned transverse/radial acoustic distances relative to the sound horizon | DESI DR2 II, arXiv:2503.14738 (2025), uses over 14 million galaxies/quasars plus Lyman-alpha BAO. Joint data combinations show a model-dependent preference for evolving dark energy. | Predict H(z), distance integrals and the drag-epoch sound horizon consistently with early-universe dynamics. BAO changes in dark-energy fits do not by themselves establish a different galactic DM force. Preserve covariance and distinguish dark matter from dark energy. |
| Large-scale growth and matter power | Galaxy/quasar power-spectrum shape and redshift-space anisotropy, combined with lensing and CMB | DESI 2024 VII, arXiv:2411.12022 / JCAP 07 (2025) 028, fits full-shape clustering including redshift-space distortions. It separately constrains phenomenological modifications to matter clustering and lensing. | Predict time- and scale-dependent perturbation growth, matter transfer, tracer bias, velocities and lensing potentials; account for nonlinear/selection modeling. A galaxy snapshot fit does not determine P(k,z) or the gravitational slip relating motion and light deflection. |

In weak-field metric language, slow-body dynamics probe one potential gradient while lensing probes a combination of two metric potentials. Thus predicting an acceleration curve does not automatically predict light bending. This is the methodological reason to require a relativistic/dynamical completion, not merely a demand for an additional empirical coefficient.

Skordis & Zlosnik (2021), arXiv:2007.00082 / PRL 127, 161302, illustrate that such a completion is possible to investigate: their proposed relativistic theory combines MOND-like quasistatic phenomenology with calculated CMB and linear matter power spectra. This is a useful counterexample to claiming that a successful cosmological completion is logically impossible. Their paper is a particular additional field theory with additional content, not a proof that an arbitrary acceleration fit inherits the same predictions or passes all observations.

## Immediate asymptotic calculation already completed

`solar_asymptotic_smoke_test.json` evaluates the previously fitted galaxy quadratic using nominal central-Sun Newtonian accelerations, without fitting any Solar-System observations. It uses GM_sun=1.3271244e20 m^3/s^2 and AU=149597870700 m. At illustrative distances 1, 9.58 and 30.07 AU, the quadratic predicts acceleration ratios to Newton of 110.57, 6.768 and 2.578, respectively. These are far outside its galaxy calibration range and show that this unconstrained polynomial cannot be asserted as a universal high-acceleration force law.

The isolated standard RAR reaches the Newtonian limit numerically at these three distances. At 1000 AU its isolated boost is about 0.079%, and at 7000 AU about 56%. The last values exclude the Galactic external field, other bodies and orbital modeling; they must not be quoted as observable predictions for real Oort-cloud objects or as fits to data. Cassini and wide-binary tests require the full boundary-value/dynamical model.

## Inputs that can be measured without fitting the missing mass

| Candidate input | Independent measurement route | Identification limitation and useful test |
|---|---|---|
| Baryonic size / shape | Stellar-light profile, gas maps, disk scale length or projected half-light radius, with explicit geometry | Independent of the target dynamical mass if obtained photometrically. Deprojection, distance and M/L uncertainties remain correlated. Replace mass-defined R500 with a measured baryonic scale where appropriate; hold out whole systems. |
| Baryonic potential | Compute Phi_bar(x)=-G integral rho_bar(x')/|x-x'| d^3x' from a specified stellar+gas model, with a specified boundary/reference | This can be independent of total dynamical mass. It requires the full density distribution, not merely g_bar*r; the latter has potential units but generally is not the potential. Disk geometry, truncation and environmental mass matter. Treat inferred halo potential or escape speed as target-dependent unless separately justified. |
| Baryonic environment | Survey positions/distances and photometric stellar/gas masses produce an external Newtonian baryonic-field estimate | Chae et al. (2021), arXiv:2109.04745, explicitly compare independently reconstructed external fields with RC-inferred fields. Missing diffuse baryons dominate uncertainty. Use the independently reconstructed value as predictor; using the value optimized from the same rotation residual would not supply independent evidence. |
| Star-formation duration / recent activity | Resolved color-magnitude diagrams, independently modeled stellar populations, gas and star-formation indicators | Read, Walker & Steger (2019), arXiv:1808.06634, connect different star-formation histories with different inferred inner dwarf DM densities. This motivates a history-dependent test. It does not identify a gravitational memory kernel. Their Mstar/M200 relation also uses abundance-matched halo masses, so M200 must not be treated as a direct independent predictor. |
| Past mergers / interactions | Stellar streams, morphology and independently constrained stellar populations, with simulations for histories | These are incomplete and model-dependent proxies for a trajectory. One snapshot cannot uniquely recover the past gravitational field. A generative simulation must specify initial conditions and latent-history priors and must predict held-out systems. |

Physical memory already exists in the sense that matter configurations retain effects of past evolution. Read et al. (2019) is an empirical motivation; Pontzen & Governato (2012), arXiv:1106.0499, provide a worked mechanism in which time-dependent baryonic potentials irreversibly redistribute collisionless particles. Memory in a new gravitational law is a separate hypothesis. To avoid counting a fitted halo's assembly dependence twice, specify whether the baseline already encodes that history.

## How dimensional or memory corrections can enter a test

As a research design, use dimensionless independently defined variables: g_bar/a0, radius over photometric baryonic size, a potential divided by a fixed potential scale, external baryonic acceleration over a0, or a history statistic divided by a stated time scale. Adding variables with different units without normalizations can hide dimensional parameters. A scale introduced by normalization remains part of the model when fitted.

If a correction is added to a RAR-like baseline, require its high-acceleration limit to vanish (unless a quantitatively allowed Solar-System signal is intended), and test the joint radius/acceleration limits rather than only the limit at fixed radius. A causal memory model must state its driver, kernel, timescale, initial state and how the history is measured or inferred independently of the residual. Kernel flexibility alone is not identifiable from one present-day acceleration measurement per location. These requirements are mathematical design criteria, not an assertion that such a correction has already improved the data.

For an actual universal theory, specify equations of motion or an action and the fields/state variables, then derive each observable. The same parameter vector should feed rotation curves, dispersion profiles, lensing, Solar-System dynamics and cosmological perturbations. Those observables have different likelihoods and cannot honestly be combined as identical acceleration points. A good piecewise fit is a provisional empirical summary until the inter-regime connection earns independent predictive support.
