A43 editorial assessment and next research target -- 29 October 2026

RECOMMENDATION
Keep A43 as a separate working research paper and reproducible technical report.
Its empirical subject and substantial transfer tests do not fit naturally into
A18's geometric-harmonic proposal or A41's discreteness argument. Those papers
need at most a short cross-reference; the fitting results are not evidence that
spacetime must be discrete. Do not present A43 as a completed discovery paper.

WHAT IS WORTH RETAINING
The comparison distinguishes interpolation within an observed galaxy, prediction
of an unseen galaxy, and transfer across populations. It documents an invalid
radius-based improvement, matched tests of established halo/RAR prescriptions,
a gas-phase transfer failure, and explicit mass-budget limits for fixed positive
nonlocal kernels. The data, code and failures make these claims reviewable.
Some of these ideas are known; no literature-wide priority claim is established.

CURRENT IMPROVEMENT
The new stellar-mass NFW mapping uses three coefficients, as did the prior
baryonic-mass mapping. Disk held-out RMS is essentially unchanged, while frozen
44-dwarf RMS is 28.2% lower. Its RAR blend improves dwarf RMS 24.2% versus the
prior blend while preserving average disk performance. The prior 25-object
quality sensitivity retains the gain. These are more useful transfer results
than the earlier roughly 2% disk-only ensemble difference.

The gain is not uniform: the pure stellar rule worsens classical dwarfs and
Andromeda, and three disk feature tails. Remaining absolute dwarf errors are
large. The new branch was chosen adaptively using already inspected outcomes.
Conditional resampling excludes host correlations and reconstruction/search
uncertainty. It does not establish literature-wide superiority or a new theory.

The population-scatter branch improves the tested predictive log score, even
under a nonlinear halo-moment sensitivity, but its central disk RMS worsens.
It is a linearized/moment-matched Gaussian approximation, not a complete nonlinear
hierarchical inference or exact predictive mixture.

FOR A JOURNAL MANUSCRIPT
The current long paper is a research record. A submission should have one clear
question, for example predictive transfer of compact baryon-halo relations, and
a shorter main text with the failed exploratory branches in a technical supplement.
Establish a distinct contribution relative to published SPARC catalogs, symbolic
regression, halo-response and cross-scale studies. Include nuisance/covariance
treatment and independent external validation. Repeatedly re-partitioning SPARC
does not correct adaptive model-search bias. External expert review is valuable.

BEST NEXT EXPERIMENT
Carry the stellar-based population mapping into a full nonlinear predictive
likelihood, including concentration scatter, photometric nuisance parameters and
selection. Compare its direct predictions with the three-coefficient stellar rule
and the five-coefficient ensemble; gains in coverage alone are not point gains.
For dwarfs, replace half-mass summaries by dispersion profiles, anisotropy priors
and satellite evolution/tidal histories. Independently measured histories could
help separate initial halo state from stripping and stellar feedback, but no new
gravity memory is established. Obtain genuinely uninspected galaxies or surveys;
additional SPARC partitions cannot undo adaptive search.

The cluster hot-gas result and dwarf failures should remain mandatory external
tests; do not retune them silently or average their different observables into
a favorable aggregate. A later unified theory must specify dynamics and lensing,
and be tested on Solar-System and cosmological likelihoods.
