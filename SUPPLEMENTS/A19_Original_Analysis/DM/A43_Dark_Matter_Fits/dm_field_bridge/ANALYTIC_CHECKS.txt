Physical-source bridge: derivation and limits

1. Kernels and total mass

NLG q0(d)=alpha mu (1+mu d) exp(-mu d)/(8 pi d^2).
Constant-parameter weak-field MOG q(d)=alpha mu^2 exp(-mu d)/(4 pi d).
Dimensions: q has length^-3; alpha dimensionless; mu length^-1.
Set t=mu d. The radial integral 4 pi d^2 q(d) dd equals alpha p(t) dt,
where p_NLG=(1+t)exp(-t)/2 and p_MOG=t exp(-t). Both integrate to one.

For positive integrable baryon density rho_b of total mass M_b, Tonelli's
theorem permits exchange of the nonnegative integrals:
 integral (q*rho_b)(x) d^3x = integral rho_b(y) d^3y integral q(x-y) d^3x
                            = alpha M_b.
The same equality for the effective mass holds without spherical symmetry.
Spherical symmetry of source AND radial kernel then permits the Newtonian
shell theorem for the effective Poisson source. At a radius containing a
fraction f of baryons, g/gbar=1+M_extra(<r)/(f M_b)<=1+alpha/f.
At f=1/2 this gives 1+2alpha. This is an isolated-source theorem; other source
mass must be included when applying it to a satellite in a host field.

2. Stronger radial-decrease bound

For any nonnegative radially decreasing rho_b, the mass of rho_b inside a
fixed-radius ball is largest when that ball is centered on rho_b. One way to
see this is layer-cake decomposition of rho_b into concentric constant-density
balls: each intersection with the test ball is maximized at zero displacement.
Since q/alpha is a probability density, convolution is a weighted average of
translations. Thus M_extra(<r)<=alpha M_b(<r), and g/gbar<=1+alpha at every
radius for this source class. This is stronger than the general spherical
bound and applies to Plummer; it need not apply to a hollow spherical shell.

3. Plummer convolution without an enclosed point-mass approximation

rho_b(r)=3 M_b a^2/[4 pi (r^2+a^2)^(5/2)].
M_b(<r)=M_b r^3/(r^2+a^2)^(3/2).
kappa=rhalf/a=[2^(2/3)-1]^(-1/2).

For lengths in units of a, define F_R(d) as the fraction of a unit Plummer
source inside a ball of radius R displaced by d. Angular integration gives
 F_R(d)=(1/(2d)) integral_0^R s{[1+(s-d)^2]^(-3/2)
                            -[1+(s+d)^2]^(-3/2)} ds.
Production integration evaluates this with a stable expm1/log1p difference.
An independently derived algebraic expression is
 A=sqrt[1+(R-d)^2], B=sqrt[1+(R+d)^2], D=d^2+1-R^2,
 F_R(d)=4 R^3/[A B (A+B)(A B+D)].
It has no subtractive large-d cancellation; at d=0 it equals the ordinary
Plummer enclosed fraction. R=kappa makes F(0)=1/2.

For z=mu a, H(z)=integral_0^infinity p(t) F_kappa(t/z) dt.
Then M_extra(<rhalf)=alpha M_b H(z), g/gbar=1+2alpha H(z).
All source mass is included, even outside the test ball. Positivity gives
H>=0; radial decrease gives H<=1/2; dominated convergence gives H->1/2
as z->infinity and H->0 as z->0. Because F decreases with displacement,
H increases with z. Both kernels tend to alpha times a spatial delta as
mu->infinity; the response is the local rescaling (1+alpha) rho_b.

4. Point-source limits and a conditional obstruction

Point-source force ratios to Newtonian are:
 NLG: 1+alpha[1-(1+t/2)exp(-t)],
 MOG: 1+alpha[1-(1+t)exp(-t)], t=mu r.
At small t the excess begins alpha t/2 (NLG) or alpha t^2/2 (MOG), and both
approach 1+alpha at large t. For an isolated finite source, far enough out,
the field returns to an enhanced inverse-square law G(1+alpha)M_b/r^2.
Thus a finite positive kernel does not produce an exactly flat rotation curve
at arbitrarily large radius. Observed finite radial intervals remain the
relevant tests; this limit alone does not rule out a finite-interval fit.

If rho_b is multiplied by c at fixed geometry, the linear equation multiplies
g by c. A homologous Plummer source therefore has the same dimensionless
enhancement at fixed a regardless of M_b. This is a concrete structural
restriction, unlike a nonlinear deep-MOND response proportional to sqrt(M_b).
Mass-size correlations can affect population trends, so this observation
alone does not prove failure of any empirical galaxy relation.

5. Bound-saturating fit interpretation

In the mu->infinity limit, write D_i=gobs_i/gbar_i and b=log10(1+alpha).
The equal-weight loss is mean[(b-log10 D_i)^2], minimized at
 b=mean(log10 D_i), alpha=10^mean(log10 D_i)-1 (if this is nonnegative).
For ML2, exact optimum alpha=58.4132155891862, production fitted NLG
alpha=58.413217156110896 (relative difference2.68e-8). All selected NLG/MOG
fit sets hit the same predeclared mu upper bound. The length is consequently
not an identified finite physical scale, and the limiting model still does
not improve the reported RAR comparison.

6. Limits of application

The theorem concerns a specified source, while gobs here is an equilibrium
Wolf-type acceleration proxy, not a direct accelerometer measurement.
Stellar population masses, spherical geometry, complete baryon inventory,
unresolved binary stars, tides and host contributions matter. Bounds use
central values with M/L sensitivity; no uncertainty-marginalized exclusion
is inferred. Regularized NLG kernels with a short-distance cutoff need their
own integral/force, and different MOG coefficient prescriptions are not
identical models. A spatial convolution does not establish a temporal causal
memory law. Cosmology and relativistic light deflection require more than
this Newtonian source equation.
