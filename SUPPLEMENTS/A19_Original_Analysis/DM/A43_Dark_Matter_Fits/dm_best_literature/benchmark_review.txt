A43 literature comparison audit
Prepared 28 October 2026 (America/Los_Angeles). Literature checked through that date.

Conclusion
A43 has not established superiority over the best available models. Its 7-coefficient regularized response improves one internal, population-balanced held-out-system statistic, but underperforms its own earlier law on whole-population transfer, is less accurate than a galaxy-calibrated RAR on disks, and has not been compared to matched full-observable likelihoods of halo, relativistic, or cosmological models. There is no scientifically meaningful scalar league table across speed residuals, lensing, CMB, and ephemerides. Define the observables, nuisance freedom, population, and prediction task first.

Scale-specific reference models
1. Solar System and strong gravity: GR with the measured ordinary sources is the reference. A viable DM modification must retain its agreement with ephemerides and gravitational-wave observables. Local acceleration screening alone does not demonstrate this: a Galactic external-field quadrupole is a nonlocal observable. Park et al. 2026 finds Q2=(1.6+/-1.8)e-27 s^-2 and tensions of 3-15 sigma for the tested classical AQUAL/QUMOND interpolation families and galaxy mass models. This excludes neither every MOND completion nor every conceivable modification. The paper argues for another scale, a screening mechanism, or modified inertia. The LIGO/Virgo/KAGRA GWTC-4.0 overview finds its general tests consistent with GR; this is a strong-field target A43 does not yet predict.
2. Disk galaxies: one-scale MOND/RAR is a demanding baryon-only prediction baseline. AQUAL and QUMOND are field theories and include geometry/external fields; the algebraic RAR is not their exact answer in every geometry. Flexible halo fits are a different baseline: Li et al. 2020 fits 175 SPARC galaxies using 7 profiles and marginalizes over mass-to-light, distance, inclination, halo mass/concentration (plus Einasto shape). Cored DC14/Burkert generally fit better than NFW. These local halo parameters cannot be quietly counted as universal constants. Use both a shared-prediction benchmark and a conditional within-system interpolation benchmark.
3. Dwarf spheroidals: use Jeans/phase-space models with stellar profiles, anisotropy, binaries, tides and memberships, alongside explicit MOND external-field predictions. A single half-light mass estimator is not that likelihood. McGaugh & Milgrom 2013 provided successful a priori Andromeda dwarf dispersion predictions, a stronger design than fitting existing per-galaxy dispersions. Neither one-point two-parameter halo fits nor arbitrary per-object MOND scales are convincing predictive victories.
4. Clusters: NFW/Einasto plus measured baryons, projection, triaxiality and hydrostatic/nonthermal nuisance modelling are strong baselines. Umetsu et al. 2016 CLASH stacked lensing favors cuspy NFW/Einasto/DARKexp over single-power, cored-isothermal, Burkert alternatives; including the two-halo term improves the large-radius fit. Eckert et al. 2022 finds average X-COP NFW mass-profile deviations below 10% over a wide radial range, but individual shape diversity remains. These are fitted mass profiles, not a parameter-free gbar-to-gobs prediction. Avoid testing an NFW shape against targets reconstructed under an NFW assumption and calling it independent evidence.
5. CMB/large-scale structure: GR + cold dark matter in the LambdaCDM cosmological framework remains the broad quantitative baseline; ACT DR6 jointly with Planck, lensing and BAO fits CMB spectra and constrains cold dark matter density. DESI DR2 combined probes motivate tests of evolving dark energy; that is not evidence that a late-time acceleration curve replaces cold dark matter. A43 has no cosmological perturbation or CMB likelihood. Relativistic MOND completions exist: Skordis & Zlosnik's AeST already demonstrated CMB and linear matter-spectrum agreement. Thus 'MOND cannot fit the CMB' is too broad; 'the algebraic RAR has no CMB prediction' is accurate.

Targeted fixes worth comparing
A. MOND: do not only retune a0. Compare interpolation shape, disk geometry, measured external fields, and baryonic nuisance parameters on identical data. Existing cluster work (Kelleher & Lelli 2024) fits a cored finite-mass extra component with outer r^-4 density; it improves profiles within about 1 Mpc, while outer hydrostatic bias/EFE remain relevant. This is MOND plus missing mass, not a successful zero-extra-mass law. A 2026 IGIMF/stars-and-remnants calculation substantially reduces the integrated cluster mass deficit, but depends on stellar-population/nucleosynthesis assumptions and is not a homogeneous radial likelihood victory.
B. CDM: compare NFW with feedback-cored DC14/Burkert and SIDM including baryonic potentials. Jia et al. 2026 fits SPARC with velocity-dependent self-interactions and core evolution; SIDM rivals feedback-based CDM in that analysis. X-COP clusters constrain scattering at high velocities (Eckert et al. 2022), so velocity dependence is a physically motivated cross-scale repair. It requires actual halo evolution/density inputs, not a relabelled gbar,r curve.
C. History: Milgrom's modified-inertia models are trajectory/frequency dependent and can give different external-field effects from AQUAL/QUMOND. They can suppress inner-Solar responses without suppressing all galactic responses. Such a dynamical construction is a more substantive direction than adding a fitted age term. It still requires trajectory data, a stable action/evolution law, and independent tests.
D. Field completion: AeST and generalized QUMOND are concrete prior-art frameworks for adding scales/derivative dependence. AeST isothermal cluster solutions can exhibit a RAR excess followed by a deficit, but the 2024 cluster paper explicitly defers quantitative comparison beyond isothermal models. Those predicted transitions may guide a test; they are not evidence that A43's empirical curve has derived the field action.

Direct new memory prior art (full primary text inspected)
Berkimbayev (2026) proposes a stationary 2D K0 source convolution. Its empirically selected long-range law is Delta V^2=sqrt(G Mtot aD) Mproj(<R)/Mtot, with global aD~1.1e-10 m/s^2. Five-fold galaxy tests give median per-galaxy relative RMS 0.169 versus RAR 0.158, with a paired interval spanning zero. This differs from A43's pointwise median error metric. With matched distance/inclination/mass-to-light freedom the likelihood still favors RAR. 'Memory' is an interpretation of the stationary endpoint, not an empirically measured temporal kernel. The author explicitly states that the total source is assigned to each cataloged disk, that a multi-source normalization rule is missing, and that extrapolation to the Sun yields 8.1e-7 m/s^2 at 1 AU and is excluded. This is valuable prior art and a caution against claiming a universal memory solution. Do not copy its parameter-per-galaxy NFW residual as directly comparable with A43.

Feasible immediate numerical comparisons using current A43 inputs
- Shared RAR and published one- or two-shape-parameter MOND interpolation families use existing gbar directly; calibrate identical folds and weights, report every population.
- Two-parameter NFW and Burkert added to the fixed baryonic radial force can fit multi-radius systems and predict held-out radii. Label this conditional interpolation, not held-out-system prediction. Dwarf/BCG single-point systems cannot identify both halo parameters; do not fill a table with trivial zero-residual fits.
- A published MOG/nonlocal point-source expression applied to flattened disks is a stress approximation, not a faithful full model benchmark. Full density/source convolution is needed.
- Memory source-convolution and action-based QUMOND/AeST cannot be reconstructed from only local gbar and radius. Their independent geometric/environmental/history inputs must be assembled; otherwise the purported completion is not being tested.
- Preserve cosmological, Solar/external-field, finite-mass, monotonic-response and BTFR checks while optimizing empirical fits. Optimization cannot compensate for missing forward observables.
