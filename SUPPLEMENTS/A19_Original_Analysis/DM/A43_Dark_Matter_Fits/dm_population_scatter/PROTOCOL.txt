A43 continuation, 29 October 2026 (Los Angeles). Frozen before new fits.

QUESTION
Can a finite NFW concentration-response correction improve point predictions?
Does marginalizing a population distribution of halo states improve held-out
predictive calibration, rather than merely widening arbitrary error bars?

DATA
Same 149 SPARC galaxies and 2700 radii, original three NumPy fivefold assignments,
and four 37-galaxy photometric tails. Restore velocity errors, signed gas/star
components, distance errors and inclination errors from archived SPARC originals.
No new astronomical sample. This is exploratory reuse of inspected data.
Frozen full-SPARC point predictors transfer to 44 dwarf half-mass estimators;
no refit to dwarfs and no claim that this substitutes for a Jeans likelihood.
No new cluster/CMB/Solar-System likelihood is computed.

POINT REPAIR
NFW retains the previous reference mass mapping and finite truncation at R200.
Add one shared concentration offset K: log10(c)=0.905-0.101 log10(Mref*h/1e12)+K.
K is bounded [-0.5,+0.5], the previous A/B/C bounds are unchanged. Four coefficients.
Fit the original equal-galaxy squared log-acceleration error with three starts and
retain the exact K=0 baseline fit. Compare all original folds/tails and dwarfs.
This is a standard phenomenological concentration response, with no novelty claim.

UNCERTAINTY MODEL
First-order Gaussian marginalization, NOT full nonlinear hierarchical Bayes.
For each galaxy, C=diag((2 eV/(ln(10)V))^2+s^2)+U U^T, in log10 acceleration.
Columns of U propagate galaxy-shared distance and inclination uncertainties and
one common stellar mass-to-light perturbation (0.1 dex). Distance changes r,Re by D,
stellar/gas proxy masses by D^2, leaving nominal baryonic acceleration unchanged.
Its residual derivative includes the change in observed acceleration (1/D).
Inclination propagation uses the kinematic sin(i) correction only. Photometric
inclination covariance and gas/distance systematics beyond these columns omitted.
Stellar amplitude changes both the measured stellar field and stellar proxy mass.
A common disk+bulge multiplier is an approximation to separate stellar priors.
No predictor is adjusted using a held-out galaxy's inferred latent state.

Dutton & Maccio 2014 supply sigma_logc=0.11 dex. Where allowed, excess halo mass
(the positive second term of the existing Mref mapping) has fitted log scatter
sigma_m in [0,0.6] dex, independent of concentration. This is an empirical
conditional scatter, not the inverse of a published stellar-mass scatter.
An independent row residual s in [0.005,0.5] dex is fitted in all likelihood models.

Declared likelihood families:
1 RAR_nuisance: mean a0 and row scatter s (2).
2 RAR_offset: same plus a marginalized galaxy-wide log-acceleration offset
  tau in [0,0.5] dex (3). Descriptive scatter, not variable universal a0.
3 NFW_c: A/B/C, s; fixed published concentration scatter (4).
4 NFW_mc: A/B/C, s, sigma_m; fixed concentration scatter (5).
5 NFW_response_mc: A/B/C/K, s, sigma_m; fixed concentration scatter (6).
6 Blend_offset: freeze the previous training-only five-coefficient force blend,
  fit s and tau on training galaxies (7 total estimated global quantities).

Fit the mean per-galaxy joint Gaussian negative log density divided by that
curve's number of radii, preserving equal system weights. This is a weighted
composite marginal log score, not a full posterior or independent-row likelihood.
Use Woodbury covariance identities and direct-matrix audit. Three declared starts,
L-BFGS-B maxiter1000 and bounded Powell polish only if selected attempts fail.
Preserve all starts/failures. Compare mean log scores, unadjusted central-prediction
RMS, median speed error, and equal-galaxy 68%/95% marginal coverage. Broader intervals
are penalized by the determinant term. Do not rank likelihood fits solely against
old MSE fits. Paired whole-galaxy bootstrap conditions on fitted predictions.

AUDIT/SENSITIVITY
Check analytic derivatives by finite differences, covariance via direct Cholesky,
exact recovery of the archived zero-response NFW, and system exclusions. Quantify
first-order accuracy with nonlinear Monte Carlo for mass/concentration scatter in
all-data NFW_mc and NFW_response_mc; report mean shift and variance error. An
approximation that fails is not promoted as a validated population likelihood.
All fits use catalog error estimates conditional on the observed measurements;
errors are not new physical predictor variables. No model is added based on test
outcomes, and no claim of independent confirmation or a new gravity law is made.

Primary sources:
Dutton & Maccio 2014, https://arxiv.org/pdf/1402.7073 (0.11 dex concentration scatter).
Li et al. 2020, https://arxiv.org/html/2001.10538v2 (0.1 dex stellar priors and
catalog distance/inclination errors; their full MCMC is not reproduced here).
