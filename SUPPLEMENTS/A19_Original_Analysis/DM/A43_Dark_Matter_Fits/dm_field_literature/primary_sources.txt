[1] Milgrom, M. (2010), Quasi-linear formulation of MOND.
MNRAS 403, 886. DOI 10.1111/j.1365-2966.2009.16184.x.
https://arxiv.org/abs/0911.5464
https://arxiv.org/html/0911.5464v2
Web refs: turn132view0, turn133view2. Word limit: 200.
The field equations are Laplacian Phi_N=4piG rho and
Laplacian Phi=div[nu(|grad Phi_N|/a0) grad Phi_N]. They follow from an action
and preserve the standard conservation laws. The easily fitted algebraic
relation g=nu(g_N/a0)g_N is generally not a potential field; it is exact only
under appropriate symmetry, or for circular trajectories in relevant modified-
inertia models. This is the important distinction between a universal field law
and a fitted radial curve.

[2] Milgrom, M. (2023), Generalizations of Quasilinear MOND (QUMOND).
Physical Review D 108, 084005. DOI 10.1103/PhysRevD.108.084005.
https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.01589
https://arxiv.org/html/2305.01589v2
Web refs: turn132view3, turn134view0, turn134view1, turn139view1.
Word limit: 200 total across this source.
Equations 6-7 permit a scalar function of the Newtonian potential and its
derivatives in a two-potential action. Variation retains two Poisson solves but
changes the effective source, including higher-derivative terms. Equation 15 uses
|grad psi|^2/||Hess psi||^2 as a local squared length. Section III.3 uses
|grad psi|^2/||Hess psi|| as a potential-dimensioned proxy, explicitly distinct
from the actual local potential. These are direct prior art for a derivative/jet
route. Particular choices screen small systems, short dynamical times, or local
Solar-System external-field effects. These possibilities are model constructions,
not a demonstrated joint fit. Higher derivatives require care at discontinuities
and degenerate fields. The source explicitly warns that one shared theory need
not impose the same algebraic interpolation curve on every phenomenon.

[3] Zhao, H., and Famaey, B. (2012), Unifying all mass discrepancies with one
effective gravity law? Physical Review D 86, 067301.
DOI 10.1103/PhysRevD.86.067301.
https://arxiv.org/abs/1207.6232
https://arxiv.org/html/1207.6232v1
Web refs: turn132view1, turn134view2. Word limit: 200.
EMOND promotes the MOND acceleration scale to A0(Phi) within an AQUAL-like
action. Varying the action adds an explicit derivative-of-A0 source term; simply
substituting A0(Phi) into the algebraic MOND relation is an approximation even
in spherical symmetry. Boundary potential matters. The authors discuss bounded
potential dependence to avoid enormous strong-field corrections. This establishes
prior art for potential-dependent cross-scale response, but does not validate
A43's local g_bar*r proxy or its coefficients.

The updated DE440-based analysis gives Q2=(1.6 +/- 1.8)*10^-27 s^-2 at one
standard deviation, 40% tighter than earlier estimates. Within tested classical
MOND modified-gravity formulations and galaxy modeling assumptions, the authors
find 3-15 sigma tension with rotation-curve fits. This is a constraint on the
external-field quadrupole, which an isolated high-acceleration asymptote does
not test. It is not a blanket exclusion of every possible modified-gravity law.

AeST is a covariant action with additional scalar/vector degrees of freedom. The
authors calculate linear CMB and matter power spectra and discuss stability.
It is an example of the added dynamical content needed beyond a galaxy RAR,
not proof that a fitted A43 curve has those cosmological properties. Extra
gravitational fields carry dynamical state and can play a dark-sector role.

[6] Rahvar, S., and Mashhoon, B. (2014), Observational Tests of Nonlocal Gravity:
Galaxy Rotation Curves and Clusters of Galaxies. Physical Review D 89, 104011.
DOI 10.1103/PhysRevD.89.104011.
https://arxiv.org/abs/1401.4819
https://journals.aps.org/prd/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevD.89.104011
Web refs: turn136academia37, turn138view2. Word limit: 200.
Their causal nonlocal framework has a Newtonian limit
Laplacian Phi=4piG[rho+q*rho], with convolution kernel
q(r)=(1+mu*r)exp(-mu*r)/(4pi lambda0 r^2).
The point-source acceleration multiplier is
1+alpha-alpha(1+mu*r/2)exp(-mu*r), alpha*mu*lambda0=2.
For extended bodies the integral over the actual density is needed; replacing
the entire source by enclosed mass violates the theory's shell behavior. The
stationary spatial kernel is not a measured temporal memory history. Their two
parameters are calibrated on galaxies and considered for clusters. The adopted
kernel has finite total effective mass, so ultimate far-field acceleration
returns to an enhanced inverse-square form.

Equations 65 and 70-71 give a point-source force
g=GM/r^2 [1+alpha-alpha(1+mu*r)exp(-mu*r)],
mu=D/sqrt(M), alpha=(G_inf/G_N-1) M/(sqrt(M)+E)^2.
D, E and G_inf are common constants. This is explicit prior art for shared
mass-dependent response and transition scale. It is a spherical weak-field
construction; direct enclosed-mass substitution is not an exact treatment of
arbitrary extended geometry. Its ultimate far field is inverse-square, even if
the finite observed range can look flatter.

[8] Pizzuti, L., et al. (2025), Testing refracted gravity with the kinematics of
galaxy clusters. Astronomy & Astrophysics 698, A83.
DOI 10.1051/0004-6361/202452739.
https://doi.org/10.1051/0004-6361/202452739

The two cluster fits were individually viable, but required inconsistent values
of parameters proposed to be universal. The authors discuss geometry and
equilibrium assumptions as possible systematics. This supplies a concrete warning
that fitting separate objects with the same functional family does not establish
one common parameter set.

The CIA relation is g_obs approximately g_b+g_int, with g_int=G*a_M. The full
paper explicitly makes this interaction scale system-dependent; cluster data are
fit in separate radial-aperture subsets. Its section 4.1 says G is not universal.
For very-low-acceleration lensing a separate VIM uses halo normalization, radius
and interaction parameters. Thus broad coverage is not a demonstration of one
fixed coefficient set predicting all populations. This clarifies the comparison
in current A43, whose earlier version checked the abstract only.
