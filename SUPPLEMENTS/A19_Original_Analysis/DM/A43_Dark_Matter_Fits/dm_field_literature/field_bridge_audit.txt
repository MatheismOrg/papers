A43 field-law bridge audit -- 28 October 2026
Research notes for the parent agent; this is not a fitted gravity theory.

1. Distinguish a shared field law from one universal scalar curve

Einstein's equation is a relation among spacetime fields, supplemented by matter
equations and initial/boundary data. Its solutions are not one function of radius.
Analogously, a baryonic response law must specify how it uses the distribution
of matter, environment and, if relevant, additional state. A present-day relation
g_pred(g_bar,r) is a useful empirical target, but does not yet have that content.

Elementary consistency calculation: let g_N=-grad psi and g_trial=f(g_N) g_N.
Then curl(g_trial)=grad f cross g_N, generally nonzero. A scalar potential force
therefore cannot generally equal this vector field pointwise. One-dimensional
symmetry is an important exceptional case. QUMOND instead solves for the scalar
potential whose Laplacian equals the divergence of the trial field. This removes
its curl component and changes predictions for general geometry.

2. Check of the proposed two-invariant action (independent calculation)

Let psi=Phi_N, H_ij=partial_i partial_j psi, g_N=|grad psi|, and define
  X=(grad psi)^2/a0^2,
  Y=ell_*^2 H_ij H_ij/a0^2.
The gravitational Lagrangian density is
  L_g=-(8 pi G)^(-1) [2 grad Phi dot grad psi - a0^2 Q(X,Y)],
and the matter term is rho(v^2/2-Phi).
Assuming boundary terms vanish under variations, varying Phi yields
  Laplacian psi=4 pi G rho.
Varying psi yields
  Laplacian Phi = partial_i(Q_X partial_i psi)
                 - ell_*^2 partial_i partial_j(Q_Y H_ij).
Signs and factors are correct. The second term cannot be dropped when Q_Y != 0.
For Q(X,Y)=Q0(X) this reduces to QUMOND with nu(s)=Q0'(s^2).
Q0(X)=X gives Newtonian gravity. Q0(X)~(4/3)X^(3/4) at X->0 gives
nu(s)~s^(-1/2), the standard deep-MOND limit. This is a specialization of the
published GQUMOND class, not a new action class.

Dimensional and point-mass checks:
  ell_J=sqrt(6)*g_N/sqrt(H_ij H_ij),
  P_J=sqrt(6)*g_N^2/sqrt(H_ij H_ij),
  M_J=6*g_N^3/[G H_ij H_ij]=g_N ell_J^2/G.
For psi=-GM/r, the Hessian eigenvalues are (-2,1,1)*GM/r^3,
so H_ij H_ij=6(GM)^2/r^6, ell_J=r, P_J=GM/r and M_J=M.
For arbitrary geometry they are local derivative invariants/proxies, not enclosed
mass, absolute potential or a unique physical radius. A constant added potential
does not change them. A uniform added field does change them, so they permit an
external-field response. Their values can be undefined or divergent at H=0;
regularity must be established for Q and the physical source, not merely hidden
by choosing a tiny numerical denominator. At a force saddle g_N=0 while H may
remain finite, these proxies vanish; this is not absence of gravitational tides.

Milgrom (2023) explicitly supplies the unnormalized length and potential proxies;
the normalized mass combination above is elementary algebra of the same
invariants. No novelty claim follows from changing normalization or notation.
Radial regression on m=log10(g_bar/a_ref)+2 log10(r/kpc) does not measure H,
does not establish M_J, and does not fit Q. Replacing its m with M_J defines a
different hypothesis that requires the full derivative term and new predictions.

3. Practical next experiment

Build an action-derived solver with declared Q, using smooth spherical profiles
(e.g. Plummer/Hernquist) and then an axisymmetric disk. Verify Newtonian recovery,
point-mass asymptotics, grid/domain convergence and global momentum conservation.
Add an independently set uniform external field before estimating the Solar
quadrupole. Fit shared Q parameters to reconstructed baryonic density fields,
and compare observable velocities/dispersion/lensing through common likelihoods.
The existing 2,910-row table lacks the full baryonic density/Hessian fields for
most objects, so it cannot by itself perform this full field-law test. A spherical
approximation must be labeled and cannot be treated as an exact disk evaluation.

A useful narrower check on existing radial candidates is the inferred spherical
extra density rho_extra=(4 pi G r^2)^(-1) d[r^2(g_pred-g_bar)]/dr. Negative
values rule out interpretation as an ordinary nonnegative-density spherical halo
over that profile. They do not alone rule out modified gravity, whose inferred
phantom density can be negative. Conversely, positivity does not establish a
consistent field theory.

4. What successful endpoint would require

One fixed action and coefficient set; source/state variables inferred without
using the response residuals; initial/environmental data; conservational and
stability checks; both metric potentials for lensing; cosmological background and
perturbation predictions; Solar-System orbital and external-field constraints.
A static acceleration fit cannot inherit these predictions from GR or from a
different MOND theory merely by sharing its low-acceleration asymptote.
