Independent analytical precheck, 2026-10-28
Prepared before receiving candidate numerical results.

A. Point-source monotonicity for exponential RAR
For fixed source mass M, gb=GM/r^2, t=sqrt(gb/ae), and inferred extra mass
Mex=M/(exp(t)-1). If n=d ln(ae)/d ln(r)>-2, then
 d ln(t)/d ln(r)=-1-n/2<0,
so Mex increases with r and the point-source inferred extra density is nonnegative.
This is not a proof for arbitrary nonspherical/extended baryons or a local action.
For a positive sum, n is a positive weighted mean of the component slopes.

B. Proposed transient repair (verify exact implementation separately)
Let U=(gb/aref)(r/kpc)^2, s=(gb/aref)(r/kpc),
S=r^4/(r^4+ell^4), W=1/(1+(gb/gc)^2), and D=1/(1+r/R).
ae/aref=C0+D[C1 U^-alpha S W + C2 s^beta].
For an isolated point source U is constant, s proportional to r^-1,
 dlnS/dlnr=4(1-S), dlnW/dlnr=4(1-W),
 dlnD/dlnr=-(1-D).
The inverse-mass leg has slope >=-1; the positive-potential leg has slope
 -beta-(1-D)>-(1+beta)>-2 if beta<1. The constant leg has zero slope.
Therefore the condition in A holds. All extra ae legs vanish as r -> infinity,
so ae -> aref C0 and V_infinity^4=GM aref C0 for every fixed source mass.
For a smooth baryon core gb proportional to r, U proportional to r^3,
S proportional to r^4: inverse leg proportional to r^(4-3alpha), regular
for alpha<4/3. Positive leg proportional to r^(2beta). For alpha<=.95 and
beta>=0, ae is finite and force g tends to zero (normally proportional sqrt(r)).
At fixed r and gb->0, even the inverse-mass ae divergence produces
g proportional to gb^((1-alpha)/2) ->0 for alpha<1.
At fixed r and gb->infinity, ae/gb ->0 if alpha>=0,beta<1 and W is present
on the inverse-mass term; exponential interpolation makes absolute excess vanish.

C. Proposed potential taper (verify exact implementation separately)
Replace D by T=s/(s+s0) and apply W to both nonconstant legs.
For point sources dlnT/dlnr=-(1-T), so the above n>-2 argument also holds.
As r->infinity, T->0 and both extra legs vanish, restoring common BTFR.
At fixed r, gb->0, inverse-mass leg now scales as gb^(1-alpha) (times S),
which tends to zero for alpha<1; ae tends to finite baseline.
At a smooth core the inverse-mass leg scales r^(6-3alpha), also regular.
As gb->infinity at fixed r, W forces both added legs to zero for beta<2.

D. Published MOND interpolations need distinct solar limits
The simple function g=0.5 gb+sqrt(0.25gb^2+a0gb) has g-gb ->a0 at high gb.
It has a Newtonian RELATIVE limit but not a vanishing absolute excess.
The standard mu function has g-gb approximately a0^2/(2gb), which vanishes.
The generalized nu_n=[(1+sqrt(1+4 z^-n))/2]^(1/n), z=gb/a0,
has g-gb approximately (a0/n) z^(1-n). Thus n>1 is needed to vanish;
n=1 is simple, n=2 is standard. An isolated limit alone says nothing
about the external-field quadrupole constraints from Cassini.

E. Halo normalization
For rho_NFW=rho_s/[x(1+x)^2], M=4pi rho_s rs^3 f_N(x),
f_N=ln(1+x)-x/(1+x). For rho_B=rho_s/[(1+x)(1+x^2)],
M=4pi rho_s rs^3 f_B(x),
f_B=0.5ln(1+x)+0.25ln(1+x^2)-0.5 atan(x).
Therefore g=A f(x)/[x^2 f(1)] correctly normalizes A=g(rs).
The derivative identities are f_N'=x/(1+x)^2 and
f_B'=x^2/[(1+x)(1+x^2)], ensuring positive density.

Post-implementation confirmation
The actual potential-taper code applies S to both nonconstant legs, not only
the inverse-mass leg. This adds another nonnegative 4(1-S) log-radius slope
to the positive leg along a point source, preserving the proof above.
The transient formula matches B exactly.

Independent numerical diagnostics reproduce all reported counts: transient has
zero negative response intervals on the specified fixed-radius grid and zero
negative point-source extra-density intervals; taper has 683 negative response
intervals despite zero negative point-source density intervals; the two log bumps
have 2,900 negative response intervals and 613 negative extra-density intervals.
No absence of failures on a grid is promoted to an all-domain monotonicity proof.
The monotonic point-source extra-density proof is narrower than a general field
law and does not imply positivity for arbitrary extended sources or conservation.

The restoring scale R is about 308 kpc for the all-data transient fit. Low-mass
systems may reach their formal common normalization only at radii well beyond
observed flat rotation curves. Common asymptotic BTFR normalization must therefore
not be written as an observational fit to the BTFR.
