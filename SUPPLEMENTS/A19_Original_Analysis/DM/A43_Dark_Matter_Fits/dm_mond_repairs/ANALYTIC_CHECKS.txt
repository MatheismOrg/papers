Analytic checks for the seven fixed candidates (28 October 2026)

These scalar force laws are not full conservative field theories. Coordinates
U=g_b r^2/(a_ref kpc^2) and s=g_b r/(a_ref kpc) are proxies; U is not an
actual enclosed disk mass. Source geometry and external fields are absent.

For F(g,a)=g/[1-exp(-sqrt(g/a))], write t=sqrt(g/a), so extra acceleration
Delta g=g/(exp(t)-1). For fixed g<<a, F~sqrt(g*a). For t->infinity,
Delta g decreases exponentially and may vanish even if a grows sublinearly.

Published generalized family:
nu_n(y)=[(1+sqrt(1+4 y^-n))/2]^(1/n), n>0. All have nu~y^-1/2 in the
low-y limit and nu->1 for large y. In the high-y limit nu-1~y^-n/n,
so Delta g~a0^n g^(1-n)/n. Simple n=1 leaves a constant absolute residual;
standard n=2 gives zero absolute residual; n<1 has a growing absolute residual
although the fractional residual vanishes. These are benchmarks, not claims
that every shape passes Solar-System data. Asymptotic V^4=GM*a0 is common.

mixed_transient:
At fixed r>0 and g->0, a_eff~const*g^-alpha (alpha>0), so
F~g^((1-alpha)/2)->0 since alpha<=.95. The slow exponent can matter physically.
At fixed r and g->infinity, the positive leg is at most O(g^.95), the inverse
leg is O(g^(-alpha-2)), and t diverges, so both absolute and relative extra
acceleration vanish. For a smooth density core g~r, U~r^3 and s~r^2,
the inverse leg scales as r^(4-3alpha) and goes to zero; the positive leg tends
zero for beta>0 and stays finite for beta=0. Thus g_pred~sqrt(r)->0: a finite
vanishing force, not a proof of finite inferred extra density. For a fixed
isolated point mass M and r->infinity, U is constant, s~1/r, S,W->1;
both extra terms vanish as r^-1 and r^(-1-beta), so a_eff->a_ref*C0 and
V_infinity^4=GM*a_ref*C0. This repairs the earlier mass-dependent asymptotic
BTFR coefficient. It does not establish the observed finite-radius BTFR.

mixed_potential_taper:
T=s/(s+s0). At fixed r and g->0, inverse and positive terms scale as
 g^(1-alpha), g^(1+beta), respectively: both vanish and a_eff->a_ref*C0.
At fixed r and high g, W makes them scale as g^(-alpha-2) and g^(beta-2);
a_eff again tends a_ref*C0 and Delta g->0. For a smooth core, S~r^4 and T~r^2:
the inverse term scales as r^(6-3alpha), positive as r^(6+2beta); force vanishes.
For fixed M at large r, T~1/r while U is constant, so both extra terms vanish
as r^-1 and r^(-1-beta). The common asymptotic coefficient is a_ref*C0.

Point-source extra-density result, both mixed repairs:
For fixed M, g_N=GM/r^2. Let n=d ln(a_eff)/d ln r. For a positive sum, n is a
positive-weighted mean of component slopes. In mixed_transient, these slopes
are 0; 4(1-S)+4(1-W)-r/(r+R)>=-1; and -beta-r/(r+R)>-1.95.
In mixed_potential_taper they are 0; 4(1-S)+4(1-W)-(1-T)>=-1;
and 4(1-S)+4(1-W)-beta-(1-T)>-1.95.
Thus n>-2 for every allowed coefficient, and d ln t/d ln r=-1-n/2<0.
M_extra=M/(exp(t)-1) therefore increases with r: its inferred point-source
extra density is nonnegative. This does not prove positivity for arbitrary
extended sources, nor the existence/stability of a field theory.

Two log bumps:
Both finite-width Gaussians vanish as q->+/-infinity. Fixed r, low/high g;
smooth-density core q~2log r; and isolated point-source far field q~const-log r
all approach a_eff=a_ref*C0. Empty-source and central forces therefore vanish,
strong-g extra acceleration vanishes, and the far-field BTFR coefficient is
common. However, the logarithmic slope can be arbitrarily steep in a bump's
wing. Monotonic g(g_b) and nonnegative inferred point-source extra density
are not guaranteed; numerical violations must not be concealed.

All C0 normalizations must be reported relative to the common reference
1.2e-10 m/s^2. Preserving the BTFR exponent and a mass-independent normalization
is an asymptotic mathematical property, not a measured agreement with that
normalization or a fit to independent BTFR data. No EFE, Q2, binary, lensing
metric or cosmological data likelihood has been fitted by this stage.
