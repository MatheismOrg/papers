If spacetime is the causal order of discrete updates, a theory of everything is an update rule, searched shortest first since each extra bit halves its prior weight. This paper searches all 2,935 rules rewriting two connected relations into three and 3,500 sampled longer ones, reading each causal graph with three causal-set dimension tests fixed in advance with noise bands. Run oldest relations first, a 20-bit mirror pair grows two-dimensional orders; under random orders it reads near one, so update order belongs in a rule's length. No rule reads four on all three tests. The closest read four on two, with chain-based estimates 4.7 to 21 standard deviations below the four-dimensional reference. Part of the gap is a bound: an update has at most as many direct causes as its rule consumes relations. Four dimensions need large runs: an interval 200 steps tall holds about $10^8$ elements. Sequential growth escapes the bound yet fails: transitive percolation reaches four on the chain at a million elements while its midpoint drifts to 5.8. From the top, one local unitary step reproduces the Dirac equation in curved spacetime, horizon crossing and pair creation in 2,488 bits. As a proposed specification on a supplied lattice, with declared dark matter and initial data, the known laws compress to 16,272 bits. All four groups in a new 24-chain Regge audit fail the mixing screen; a curvature-squared term, which quantum matter requires, makes all four pass. A complete quantum-gravity construction remains open. Code and data are supplied.
