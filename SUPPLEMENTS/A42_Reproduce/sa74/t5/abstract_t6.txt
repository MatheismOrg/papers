Spacetime is the causal order of discrete updates, so a theory of everything is an update rule, and the search can run shortest first, since each extra bit halves a rule's prior weight. This paper searches all 2,935 rules that rewrite two connected relations into three, and samples of 3,500 longer ones, reading each causal graph with three dimension tests from causal-set theory, fixed in advance with noise bands. Run oldest relations first, a 20-bit mirror pair grows two-dimensional orders; under random orders it reads near one, so a rule's update order belongs in its length. No rule reads four on all three tests. The closest read four on two, with chains 4.7 to 21 standard deviations short of four dimensions. Part of the gap is a bound: an update has at most as many direct causes as its rule consumes relations. Sequential growth escapes the bound yet fails: transitive percolation reaches four on the chain at a million elements while its midpoint drifts to 5.8. From the top, one local unitary step reproduces the Dirac equation in curved spacetime, horizon crossing and pair creation in 2,488 bits. The full known laws, set on a supplied four-dimensional lattice and completed with dark matter and an initial state, compress to 16,272 bits; their lattice quarks keep an exact chiral symmetry, and the Planck spacing lies 43 million times inside the LHAASO Lorentz bound. Its open test is smooth spacetime from the lattice's own quantum gravity. Code and data are supplied.
