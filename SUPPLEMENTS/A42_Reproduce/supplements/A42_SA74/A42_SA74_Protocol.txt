A42 four-dimension test, fixed before the SA74 runs (October 31, 2026)

1. Readings. Each causal interval is read by the three tests of Section 3: the ordering fraction inverted with
f(d) = Gamma(d+1) Gamma(d/2) / (2 Gamma(3d/2)); midpoint scaling, d = log2(N / largest smaller half-interval);
the longest chain, L = m_d N^(1/d) with m_2 = 1.9544, m_3 = 2.0567, m_4 = 2.1443, m_5 = 2.2492, m_6 = 2.3,
interpolated linearly in d.

2. Intervals. As in Section 3: the six elements with the largest pasts; the largest intervals run to each from
the root; the inner intervals run to each from the element at 30 percent of its depth that makes the interval
largest; each holds at least 120 elements; a size series adds single intervals from the top element down to
bottom ends at 0, 10, ..., 70 percent of its depth, each of at least 60 elements.

3. Noise bands. Sprinkled intervals of flat spacetime in d = 2, 3 and 4 dimensions, with N = 120, 250, 500,
1000, 2000 and 4000 points inside, replicated 40, 40, 40, 20, 12 and 6 times with seeds 1, 2, 3, ... Each test
t gets a mean mu_t(d, N) and a standard deviation sigma_t(d, N). Between the sizes, mu and sigma are
interpolated linearly in log N; outside them the nearest size is used.

4. An interval reads d-dimensional when each of its three readings lies within 3 sigma of mu at its size.
The window of the first passes, all three readings between 3.5 and 4.5, is reported alongside.

5. A rule or growth model is a four-dimensional candidate when its largest interval of at least 1,000
elements reads four-dimensional and the two largest intervals of its size series with at least 1,000
elements both read four-dimensional, so that the readings do not drift away from four as the size grows.

6. Runs under this protocol: (a) update orders oldest-first (the first passes), newest-first, and random
with seeds 1 and 2, each to 20,000 updates, on the ten rules of the runs past 100,000 updates and the four
other near misses of the first pass; (b) transitive percolation, the simplest sequential growth, with
N = 1000, 2000, 4000 and 8000 elements and p = 0.3, 0.1, 0.03, 0.01, 0.003, 0.001 and 0.0003, seeds 1 and 2,
skipping pN above 500, with a root added below all elements as the bottom of the largest intervals;
(c) causal invariance by critical pairs: every pair of overlapping matches in a minimal state is applied
both ways, and the pair resolves if some states reached from the two results in at most two further updates
coincide up to renaming the new nodes.

7. These criteria are not changed after the results are seen. Any later change is reported as a change,
with its reason.
