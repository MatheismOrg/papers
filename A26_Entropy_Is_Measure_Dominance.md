## Abstract

Why does entropy increase? The standard answer invokes the Past Hypothesis: entropy was low at the Big Bang, and the rest follows statistically. But why was it low? Penrose puts the odds at 1 in 10^(10^123). Albert treats the Past Hypothesis as an unexplained axiom. This paper argues the question dissolves under algorithmic probability. Low-entropy states have low Kolmogorov complexity: "uniform gas at temperature T in region R" is a short description. Mid-entropy states peak in complexity: irregular configurations with no compressible pattern. The complexity curve is non-monotonic. Observers find themselves in entropy-increasing histories for the same reason they find themselves in universes with simple laws: the Simplicity Dominance Principle. The Past Hypothesis is a theorem, not a postulate.

**Keywords:** entropy, second law of thermodynamics, algorithmic probability, Kolmogorov complexity, Past Hypothesis, Simplicity Dominance Principle

## 1. The Problem

Penrose (2004) estimates the probability of our initial conditions at 1 in 10^(10^123), treating low entropy as spectacularly unlikely. Albert (2000) proposes the Past Hypothesis as a fundamental law, coordinate with the dynamical laws and the probability postulate. Carroll (2010) builds baby universes spawning low-entropy regions from high-entropy parents. Price (1996) argues the real puzzle is the asymmetry itself.

Each treats the low-entropy beginning as a brute fact. The question is backwards. Low entropy is not special. It is simple.

## 2. Specification Cost

A billiard break. One cue ball, one velocity, a few bits. The laws compute the rest. Balls scatter. Energy dissipates. Entropy increases. The specification is compact.

Reverse it. Every air molecule, every vibration in the felt, every photon of radiated heat must carry exact momenta to converge on the triangle. The specification is the full microstate of the environment. The laws permit it. The cost to specify it is the universe.

Under algorithmic probability, the measure over structures is P(S) proportional to 2^(-K(S)). Each additional bit of specification halves the measure. Entropy-decreasing histories require thousands of additional bits. Their measure is not slightly lower. It is exponentially, astronomically lower.

## 3. The Complexity Curve

Penrose's 1 in 10^(10^123) counts microstates: how many arrangements correspond to our low-entropy initial conditions versus all possible arrangements. This is the wrong measure. The relevant measure is Kolmogorov complexity: how long is the shortest description?

Consider a gas of N particles.

**Low entropy (ordered):** All particles in the left half of a box, uniform velocity distribution at temperature T. Description: "N particles, left half, Maxwell-Boltzmann at T." A few parameters. K is low.

**Mid entropy (partially mixed):** Particles distributed in a specific irregular pattern, some clustered, some dispersed, no symmetry. No short description captures the configuration. Every particle's position and momentum must be listed individually. K is high.

**High entropy (equilibrium):** Particles uniformly distributed, maximum entropy. Description: "N particles, uniform, Maxwell-Boltzmann at T." A few parameters. K is low again.

The complexity curve peaks at intermediate entropy. This is the key insight Penrose's microstate-counting misses. Low-entropy states and equilibrium states are both simple to describe. The low-entropy beginning is not a 1-in-10^(10^123) coincidence. It is a short description. Short descriptions dominate the measure.

Among simple states, low-entropy ones are favored for observation because they have the richest forward structure: entropy gradients, record formation, computation, observers. Equilibrium is equally simple but sterile.

## 4. Compression and the Computational Arrow

Entropy increase is many-to-one. Many distinct microstates produce similar macrostates as systems equilibrate. The mapping from microstates to macrostates is compression: many inputs, fewer distinguishable outputs. Many-to-one has no inverse function. The forward direction is the computable direction (Bernstein, 2026b).

The computational arrow explains why observers model forward. The entropy argument explains why the universe they observe has the statistical properties it does. Both are consequences of the same asymmetry: forward is cheap to specify; backward requires the universe.

## 5. The Past Hypothesis as Theorem

Albert (2000) adds an axiom. The Simplicity Dominance Principle derives what Albert stipulates:

All consistent mathematical structures exist. Their measure follows algorithmic probability. Low-entropy initial conditions have low K. Histories from low-entropy states under simple laws have lower total specification cost than alternatives. Observers require entropy gradients. These histories dominate the measure among observer-containing structures.

The Past Hypothesis is not a brute fact about our universe. It is a statistical consequence of measure dominance.

## 6. Objections

**Boltzmann brains.** If equilibrium states are simple, why not fluctuate a brain? Because the fluctuation itself requires enormous specification: a particular arrangement of 10^26 particles instantiating a self-modeling mind. A universe that evolves observers from a low-entropy beginning through chemistry has lower total K than a universe at equilibrium that fluctuates a brain. SDP selects against Boltzmann brains.

**Maximum entropy beginnings.** Equilibrium has low K but no gradient. No gradient, no computation, no records, no observers. The simplest observer-containing structures begin at low entropy.

**Anthropic reasoning.** Anthropic arguments say observers need certain conditions and note we have them. SDP assigns quantitative measure: it predicts which conditions overwhelmingly dominate, not merely which are compatible. The second law is not consistent with observers. It is the dominant observation.

**Entropy-decreasing universes.** They exist. All consistent structures do. Their measure is exponentially suppressed. The second law holds because entropy-decreasing histories are measure-zero in the relevant limit, not because they are impossible.

## 7. Relation to Other Arrows

The computational arrow: minds model forward because many-to-one has no inverse (Bernstein, 2026b). The entropy arrow: entropy-increasing histories dominate the measure. The cosmological arrow: expansion from low-entropy states follows from measure dominance over initial conditions.

One root: specification cost asymmetry under algorithmic probability. Forward is cheap. Backward is expensive. Carroll (2010) builds a multiverse mechanism to generate low-entropy regions. SDP needs no mechanism. The measure over mathematical structures already provides it.

## 8. Conclusion

You observe entropy increasing for the same reason you observe simple laws: simplicity dominates. The second law is a selection effect, not a dynamical law. The Past Hypothesis is a theorem, not a postulate. Low-entropy beginnings are simple, not special.
