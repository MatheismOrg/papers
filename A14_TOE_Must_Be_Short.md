# The Theory of Everything Must Be Short

**Gary Abraham Bernstein**
Independent Researcher
ORCID: 0009-0009-1761-2867

## Abstract

The search for a theory of everything has been framed as a unification problem: find one framework that reconciles quantum mechanics and general relativity. This paper argues it should be framed as a compression problem. Under algorithmic probability, the correct theory of everything is the shortest description that reproduces all observation. The Standard Model plus general relativity requires approximately 390 bits of specification: 26 free parameters, each to measured precision, plus the gauge group and field content. Any candidate TOE that requires more specification is disfavored exponentially, not marginally. String theory's landscape selection requires over 3,000 bits. The Simplicity Dominance Principle predicts the TOE is shorter than the Standard Model, not longer. Unification that increases specification cost is not progress. It is movement in the wrong direction.

**Keywords:** theory of everything, Kolmogorov complexity, algorithmic probability, unification, Simplicity Dominance Principle, Standard Model

## 1. The Wrong Framing

Weinberg (1992) envisioned a final theory as the endpoint of unification: electromagnetism and the weak force became electroweak; the strong force joined via QCD; gravity remains outside. The program assumes the TOE will be found by combining existing frameworks into a larger one.

This assumption has no justification under algorithmic probability. Unification is valuable only if the combined description is shorter than the separate ones. Electroweak unification succeeds: one gauge group SU(2) x U(1) replaces two separate force descriptions, reducing total specification. The Standard Model succeeds: SU(3) x SU(2) x U(1) with three generations of fermions, specified in fewer bits than listing each particle and interaction separately.

But unification can also fail by this measure. If combining gravity with the Standard Model requires introducing 10 extra dimensions, a landscape of 10^500 vacua, and a selection mechanism, the total specification has increased enormously. The theory is longer, not shorter. Under SDP, it is disfavored.

## 2. Bit Counts

The Standard Model has 26 free parameters: 6 quark masses, 3 lepton masses, 3 neutrino masses (or mass differences), 3 mixing angles and 1 CP-violating phase for quarks, 3 mixing angles and 1-3 CP-violating phases for leptons, the strong coupling constant, the Higgs mass and vacuum expectation value, the cosmological constant, and the QCD vacuum angle. Each measured to finite precision. At current experimental precision, the total specification is approximately 390 bits.

General relativity adds 2 parameters: Newton's gravitational constant and the cosmological constant (already counted above). The field equations follow from the Einstein-Hilbert action, which is the simplest generally covariant scalar. Its specification cost is near zero given the assumption of a metric manifold.

The combined SM+GR description: approximately 390 bits plus the gauge group and spacetime structure.

String theory adds: selection of one vacuum from approximately 10^500 candidates (at least 1,661 bits to index the vacuum alone), flux quanta, moduli stabilization parameters, and SUSY-breaking mechanism. Conservative total: 3,000 to 4,700 additional bits (Bernstein, 2026g).

Under P(S) proportional to 2^(-K(S)), each additional bit halves the prior probability. At 3,000 additional bits, string theory's prior is lower by a factor of 2^3,000. This is not a marginal disfavoring. It is an extinction-level prior penalty.

## 3. The TOE Is Shorter

If SDP is correct, the theory of everything has lower Kolmogorov complexity than the Standard Model. Not zero — the 26 parameters still need specification — but lower. This means the free parameters are not free. They follow from a shorter generating rule.

What would this look like? The 26 parameters would be derivable from a smaller set. The gauge group SU(3) x SU(2) x U(1) would follow from a simpler structure rather than being specified by hand. The three generations of fermions would be a consequence, not an input.

Several approaches aim in this direction. Wolfram's hypergraph program seeks rules so short that the Standard Model emerges from computation (Wolfram, 2020). Geometric approaches derive gauge structure from the metric bundle over a 4D manifold, which provides 14 dimensions at zero additional specification cost (Bernstein, 2026m). Asymptotic safety derives quantum gravity from the existing framework without adding dimensions or parameters.

The common thread: progress means shorter descriptions. Any approach that adds specification — extra dimensions, landscapes, selection mechanisms, new symmetries that must then be broken — moves away from the TOE, not toward it.

## 4. Implications

**For string theory:** The landscape is not a feature. It is a fatal specification cost. A theory that cannot predict which vacuum we inhabit without 1,661+ bits of additional input has failed the compression test. No amount of mathematical elegance compensates for a 2^3,000 prior penalty.

**For model selection:** Theory comparison should use minimum description length, not mathematical beauty, unification aesthetics, or consistency alone. Many consistent theories exist. The one with the shortest total description — parameters plus framework — dominates the measure.

**For experimentalists:** If the TOE is shorter than the Standard Model, the 26 parameters contain redundancy. Relationships between parameters — mass ratios, coupling constant coincidences, generation structure — are clues to the generating rule. These relationships are where to look.

**For Hawking's model-dependent realism:** Hawking and Mlodinow (2010) argued there may be no single TOE, only a patchwork of models covering different domains. Under SDP, this is the second-best outcome: a patchwork has higher total K than a single short description. The TOE exists. It is the shortest description. Whether we can find it is an empirical question. That it exists is a mathematical one.

## 5. Objections

**The TOE might be incompressible.** If the 26 parameters are algorithmically random — no shorter description generates them — then the Standard Model is already the TOE. This is possible. But the regularity we observe (three generations, approximate coupling constant unification at high energies, mass hierarchies with suggestive patterns) points toward a shorter generating description.

**Minimum description length is relative to a reference machine.** Kolmogorov complexity depends on the choice of universal Turing machine. But the invariance theorem guarantees that complexities under different machines differ by at most a constant. For descriptions of 390+ bits, the constant is negligible. The ranking is robust.

**Beauty matters.** Beauty in physics has historically tracked simplicity, not coincidentally. Dirac's equation is beautiful because it is short: one equation replacing Schrödinger's for relativistic electrons, with antimatter as a free consequence. Beauty that adds specification cost — beautiful extra dimensions, beautiful landscapes — is aesthetics divorced from the measure.

## 6. Conclusion

The theory of everything is a compression problem. The correct theory has the shortest description reproducing all observation. The Standard Model at 390 bits sets the target. String theory at 3,000+ bits fails. Progress means finding shorter descriptions, not grander unifications. Unification that increases specification cost is movement in the wrong direction.
